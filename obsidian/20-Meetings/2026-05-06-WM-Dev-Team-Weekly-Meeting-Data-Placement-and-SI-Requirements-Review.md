# WM Dev Team Weekly Meeting: Data Placement and Submission Infrastructure Requirements Review

## Summary

This recording covers three back-to-back discussions held in the same Zoom room, all centered on drafting requirements for the future CMS Workload Management (WM) system built on [[DIRAC]]/[[DIRACX]], ahead of an upcoming CMS retreat.

The first part (the regular "New WM Dev team weekly meeting," ~16:20–17:02, with Alan Malta Rodrigues, Hasan Ozturk, Panos Paparrigopoulos, Andrea Piccinelli, Eric Vaandering, and Marco Mascheroni) reviewed a shared requirements document on **data placement and Rucio rule management**. The group discussed whether users should own data placement individually versus using centrally-managed [[Rucio]] accounts (leaning centrally-managed, as CRAB already does for input and Tier-3 does separately), how to phrase output-data requirements without relying on CMS/CRAB-specific jargon like "announced," and the risk that output data could be lost if not locked in Rucio before it is confirmed correct (since Rucio evicts unlocked/dynamic disk space on an LRU basis). A long technical discussion covered how WM currently manages block-level vs. container-level Rucio rules, the limitations of Rucio's one-to-one parent-child rule relationship for a "many block-level rules → one container-level tape rule" use case, whether Rucio protects "source" replicas sufficiently (it does not, to the degree needed), and whether WM needs to track rule metadata locally at all versus querying Rucio directly (potentially via a workflow-ID tag embedded in rule metadata, an idea from Eric Vaandering). The group also discussed a recurring operational pain point: a single unavailable file in a large block can block an entire workflow, and operators currently work around this by invalidating files — the group agreed a manual/forceable override for partial-block processing was needed, while defaulting to full-block availability. A checksum-verification requirement for files written to storage was also discussed, with Eric Vaandering noting that a "merge at destination" model (as DIRAC+Rucio default to) surfaces transfer problems earlier and may reduce (but not eliminate) the need for this check.

The second part (~17:03–18:03, joined by Stefano Belforte and Vijay, with Andrea Piccinelli and Marco Mascheroni continuing, Katy Ellis briefly present) reviewed a separate document of **CRAB/DMWM-workshop-derived requirements**, in preparation for presenting them as CMS-specific input at the retreat. The central topic was whether to keep the CRAB client (with an internal translation layer to DIRAC) or move users to a native DIRAC client/CWL-based submission, with Stefano Belforte arguing strongly for the latter given the CRAB client's long-term maintenance burden and its current hard coupling to CMSSW (it needs a CMSSW Python environment to parse the user's parameter-set file). Other points covered CRAB configuration-schema mapping to a DIRAC-compatible format, CMS-specific OIDC-based auth/authz and the user/group/superuser question (groups are deliberately not used today due to membership-maintenance overhead, per Stefano), CMSSW environment provisioning vs. support for arbitrary/non-CMSSW payloads on worker nodes, and CMS data access via AAA/XRootD fallback, including whether users should be able to force jobs to run only where data already resides (an existing, disputed "ignore locality"-style override).

The third part ("SI discussion," from ~18:03, joined by Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo and Todor) focused specifically on **Submission Infrastructure (SI) requirements**: preserving the pilot-job/late-binding model, fair share/quotas/groups, and a substantial, unresolved debate between Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo and Alan Malta Rodrigues/Marco Mascheroni about whether WM should materialize/push as many jobs as possible into schedulers as fast as possible (Antonio's position, framed around not artificially throttling SI's ability to acquire and exploit resources, including sudden bursts) versus prioritizing job diversity and treating job bookkeeping/state-change costs in upper WM layers as a real constraint (Alan/Marco's position). This was explicitly left unresolved as a CMS-wide design trade-off. The group also discussed steady pool pressure and pilot draining/defragmentation costs, the need for a clean boundary/decoupling between the WM orchestration layer and the Condor-based job-scheduling/resource-management layer, and began discussing a flexible pilot backfill mechanism when the transcript ends mid-discussion.

## Decisions / Conclusions

- Centrally-managed Rucio accounts (not per-user data ownership) should be kept for data placement, consistent with CRAB's current input-rule account and Tier-3's separate account — no objection raised.
- The data-placement requirement should explicitly cover production as well as user/private analysis, not just the latter.
- Wording that relies on the CRAB/CMS-specific concept of a dataset being "announced" should be replaced with more generic, system-agnostic language (e.g., tied to a dataset being "closed"/finalized) so the requirement is understandable to teams unfamiliar with CRAB, such as the DIRAC team — Alan Malta Rodrigues to rework this.
- Relying solely on "once we are confident the data is correct" as the trigger for the first Rucio lock/rule was judged risky on its own: without an earlier lock, output data can be lost because Rucio evicts unlocked data from dynamic/disk space on a least-recently-used (LRU) basis.
- Rucio does not protect "source" replicas to the degree WM would need (confirmed by Panos Paparrigopoulos and Hasan Ozturk, based on prior investigation).
- Implementation details for output-data tape-rule cleanup (block-level vs. container-level rules, who executes the cleanup logic) were explicitly deferred as "to be decided," while the desired functional behavior — cleaning up disk-level block rules once the corresponding tape rule succeeds — was agreed as the target.
- Goal for output data placement: a flexible, policy-driven system configurable at different scopes (Tier 0, central production, private Monte Carlo, private user analysis), rather than the current rigid, one-size-fits-all approach — though Alan Malta Rodrigues cautioned that full flexibility has practical limits and that WM cannot delegate all data-management decisions to the DM system, since some decisions depend on information only WM has.
- For stuck/partial-block processing: the default behavior should remain "wait for the full block to be available," but the system must provide an explicit, forceable override to process what's already on disk (with user notification), rather than relying on the current workaround of invalidating individual files.
- Checksum verification of files written to storage was judged a sound requirement in principle; its necessity may be reduced (not eliminated) if CMS adopts a "merge at destination site" model, since problems would then surface earlier in the pipeline — this trade-off was not resolved.
- CVMFS availability on a worker node is provided by site admins (or the started image) and only checked (not mounted) by the pilot before it starts; CMSSW environment setup itself is performed by the JobWrapper, not the pilot.
- The crab client currently must run inside a CMSSW environment because it needs to parse the user's CMSSW parameter-set (pset) to expand configuration and extract the list of output files — identified as a key reason to decouple any future client from CMSSW.
- CMS/CRAB deliberately does not use group-based accounting today; per Stefano Belforte this is a social/maintenance-burden choice (group membership tends to only grow, never shrink), not a technical limitation — group-based authorization remains technically feasible if the community wants it.
- Job overflow (running a job away from its data and reading remotely via AAA/XRootD) already exists and works; users can currently force jobs to run only where data already resides ("ignore locality"-style override) — Stefano Belforte considers this override dangerous/a legacy mistake that should not exist going forward, though it was not formally removed as a requirement in this meeting.
- Point 5/"steady pool pressure" was clarified as distinct from point 3 (materialization speed): it concerns efficient use of already-acquired resources (avoiding losses from pool contraction/draining and pilot fragmentation), not maximizing acquisition; Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo suggested it should be phrased as a benefit/goal rather than a strict must-have.
- A clean boundary/decoupling between the workflow-materialization/orchestration layer and the Condor-based job-scheduling/resource-management layer was agreed as a genuine requirement, so each layer can scale independently on its own relevant parameters (Todor); Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed rephrasing this around "decoupling WM from Condor" plus the ability to talk to any Condor instance via a REST interface regardless of location.
- Job checkpointing and job eviction support is confirmed as a separate must-have requirement (already captured elsewhere in the document, referenced as item 9/9.9).
- On the CRAB-client-vs-native-DIRAC-client debate, discussion leaned toward the "transparent" migration approach as the starting basis for the requirements document (per Vijay), i.e., preserving the CRAB CLI/API surface where feasible, while several participants (notably Stefano Belforte) argued the underlying implementation should not try to keep maintaining the current Python crab client long-term, favoring adoption of a new/native DIRAC client with thin CRAB-emulating wrapper scripts as a fallback bridge. This remained a discussion/recommendation, not a finalized CMS-wide decision — Andrea Piccinelli noted it would need buy-in from higher-level management/community.
- Whether WM should aggressively materialize/push jobs into schedulers as fast as possible, versus limiting materialization due to the cost of job bookkeeping in upper layers, was explicitly left unresolved as a broader CMS design trade-off requiring input beyond this meeting.

## Discussion

### Data placement and Rucio rule management (WM Dev weekly meeting)

- Input data: consensus to keep centrally-managed Rucio accounts (as CRAB and Tier-3 already do) rather than per-user data ownership, for simpler quota/policy management (Hasan Ozturk, Panos Paparrigopoulos).
- Output data: proposal that data should only be committed/registered to Rucio once WM is confident it is correct/final, to avoid registering data that later turns out wrong — but Alan Malta Rodrigues raised the counter-risk that unlocked data can be evicted by Rucio (LRU-based eviction of dynamic disk space, confirmed by Panos Paparrigopoulos in response to a question from Marco Mascheroni) before that point is reached, so a lock needs to exist earlier than "announce."
- Current problem (Hasan Ozturk): output containers are currently kept fully on tape without releasing the corresponding block-level disk rules once blocks reach tape; requirement should be to unlock/clean up those disk-level block rules as blocks complete their move to tape. Panos Paparrigopoulos noted this connects to item 4 of the document.
- Rucio rule mechanics: rules are currently created at block level but managed/waited-on at container level (WM waits for the whole dataset before deleting all block-level rules) — Eric Vaandering suggested block-level tape rules as an alternative to container-level consolidation.
- Rucio does not currently protect "source" replicas to the extent WM needs (Panos Paparrigopoulos, Hasan Ozturk).
- Hasan Ozturk proposed a possible future [[Rucio]] feature: parent-child rule relationships (where the old/parent rule persists until the new/child rule succeeds) could in principle handle safe data-location transitions, but Rucio's parent-child relation is currently one-to-one, whereas this use case needs many-to-one (many block-level rules feeding one container-level tape rule) — not currently supported.
- Whether block-level tape-rule approval would overburden site admins was raised as an open question; Eric Vaandering believed most such approvals are already automated.
- RSE expressions (document point 2): both input and output should always be able to target multiple RSEs via generic RSE expressions. Current gap (Hasan Ozturk): input rules currently just reuse the workflow's site whitelist as the RSE expression rather than a genuinely independent/generic expression. Alan Malta Rodrigues hopes site whitelists are phased out in the future WM (possibly replaced by a disabled-site list), though expects user analysis will still need an override.
- Alan Malta Rodrigues flagged (as a forward-looking note, not a settled requirement) that if an RSE expression spans multiple sites and Rucio later repositions the data, WM currently has no mechanism to detect/handle that; Hasan Ozturk noted there is already a Rucio feature request for "re-evaluating rules" that might cover this, but it's unclear whether/how Rucio will implement it.
- Point 3 (disk rule cleanup granularity): agreed block-level cleanup is needed for both input and output where possible. Tier 0 and user-analysis specifics were left unresolved — Panos Paparrigopoulos noted user-analysis workflows often only touch a subset of blocks in a dataset, suggesting smarter, finer-grained locking could help, but no concrete proposal was reached; Tier 0 specifics were explicitly flagged as outside Hasan/Panos's area of expertise.
- Point 4 (manual overrides for stuck workflows): Hasan Ozturk described the recurring pain point of a single unavailable file blocking an entire block-level workflow (current system supports partial-input processing at container level but not block level, forcing manual file invalidation as a workaround). Agreed: default to waiting for full block availability, but the system should expose a way to force processing of what's available, paired with proper notification to the requester. Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed involving PPD/operations experience and asked for a concrete list of current problems (only one concrete example was given: the single-file-blocks-a-block case).
- Point 5 (rule tracking / RSE-expression updates): Hasan Ozturk questioned why WM keeps its own local record of Rucio rules it created rather than always querying Rucio. Alan Malta Rodrigues suggested this may relate to rule-ID changes (e.g., after a destination change, which generates a new rule ID that WM no longer recognizes as its own, so it stops cleaning it up) and to auditing/tracking who acted on a given DID. Eric Vaandering proposed embedding a workflow ID in Rucio rule metadata so WM could query Rucio directly per workflow instead of maintaining local state; Panos Paparrigopoulos agreed this could work if rules always carry the workflow ID. Alan Malta Rodrigues raised a caveat: Rucio deduplicates rules by an RSE-expression/DID/account uniqueness triplet, so a single rule could actually be relied on by multiple workflows, which metadata tagging alone would not reveal — impact judged unclear. Discussion also touched on Rucio's data-location model differing from FTS in that rules can exist at multiple DID levels (container, dataset, etc.), all of which may need checking to determine full data location, with a possible cost in extra load on the DM/Rucio side (Eric Vaandering confirmed overlapping rules are possible).
- Point 6 (checksum verification of output files, discussed with limited time remaining): judged a sound requirement by Alan Malta Rodrigues, though possibly storage-technology/GFAL-utility dependent. Eric Vaandering noted that DIRAC+Rucio's default behavior of moving intermediate output into Rucio (and transferring it to a merge location) very quickly means integrity problems would likely be caught earlier anyway. Hasan Ozturk maintained the check is still needed specifically for the worker-node-disk-to-site-disk transfer step, distinct from writing to tape. Marco Mascheroni raised a related, unresolved point: whether CMS should move toward doing all merging at the destination site (different from current practice), which would also affect how important this check is — described by Alan Malta Rodrigues as "a different beast" and left open. Eric Vaandering framed this as an instance of a general question from the Rucio migration: whether to reshape adopted software to match CMS's current model, or adapt CMS's model to the tool's native behavior.
- The first part of the meeting ended with two additional points (8 and 9) raised live during discussion, which Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed formalizing via document follow-up rather than in the live meeting; a further dedicated meeting was floated (tentatively later in the week or early the following week) but not firmly scheduled, given the upcoming retreat.

### CRAB/DMWM requirements review (with Stefano Belforte, Vijay)

- Framing: the document distinguishes a "transparent" migration path (same CRAB interface/API/client) from a more disruptive path where CMS accepts a materially different, DIRAC-native system. Vijay indicated the document as currently written favors the transparent approach; the group proceeded on that basis.
- CRAB client compatibility layer: Alan Malta Rodrigues asked whether the plan is to keep maintaining the crab client package (translating CRAB CLI calls into DIRAC REST calls internally) or move users to DIRAC's own tooling. Stefano Belforte argued against maintaining the current client long-term: it binds CMS to Python, is difficult to modify/extend, requires reverse-engineering an underspecified REST interface, and — critically — requires running inside a CMSSW environment just to parse the user's pset and extract the output file list. He proposed a thin wrapper around DIRAC's CLI that emulates key crab commands (submit/status/kill) as a fallback/bridge, with the community being asked to adopt the new native tool as the primary path, accepting a period of user disruption/retraining. Marco Mascheroni agreed that keeping compatibility would effectively mean rewriting the client from scratch anyway once the REST interface and scheduler assumptions change.
- Practical detail: crab client is deployed via CVMFS as an "external," with all CMSSW releases sharing the same crab code; this currently constrains crab client to Python 2.7 compatibility as a side effect (Stefano Belforte). A newer generation of the client already uses a static Go binary internally for some operations (e.g., "goclient") specifically to avoid CMSSW environment conflicts (e.g., GFAL not working correctly inside the CMSSW environment).
- CRAB configuration schema: the requirement identified is understanding the gap between a CRAB submission's configuration payload and what a DIRAC-based client/server would accept, and whether/how to provide a conversion tool for users' existing CRAB configuration files (as was done previously for the CRAB2→CRAB3 migration). Stefano Belforte noted current CRAB configs are often copy-pasted and contain stale/unnecessary settings, complicating any straightforward automatic conversion; this was raised as something needing further investigation once hands-on experience running jobs on a DIRAC instance is available (referenced: Valentin's not-yet-completed DIRAC installation).
- CMS-specific authentication/authorization: agreed as a needed move away from proxies toward full OIDC token adoption. Stefano Belforte confirmed the current model has users and superusers but no groups, by deliberate choice — group membership maintenance is a "social," not technical, problem (people get added but rarely removed, as seen historically with physics-convener-based groups). A single-user-per-submission model must be preserved regardless (one user cannot affect another's jobs by mistake, aside from operator/superuser intervention).
- CMSSW environment provisioning: confirmed as a common requirement across all three scopes (Tier 0, central production, user analysis) — the system must be able to set up a requested CMSSW release on the worker node. Clarified: CVMFS availability is provided by the site/image and checked (not mounted) by the pilot; CMSSW environment setup itself is done by the JobWrapper. Separately discussed: whether/how to support arbitrary, non-CMSSW executables (e.g., using local CUDA/Singularity images, or entirely different environments). Stefano Belforte cautioned that once arbitrary code execution is allowed, essentially anything can be run, and cited a hypothetical abuse case (mass `wget` of a large payload from CERN across many jobs simultaneously) as the type of risk to guard against; the working assumption remains that the system sets up a default environment first, which users can unset if they need something different, similar to today.
- CMS data access via AAA/XRootD fallback: currently fully automated/delegated to CMSSW's pool-source behavior, with no user-facing opt-out for CMSSW-based jobs (Stefano Belforte); for non-CMSSW Python-based analysis, users currently roll their own remote-access logic (e.g., inserting a global XRootD redirector in front of logical file names) since there's no equivalent built-in support. Discussion touched on whether WM should trigger input-data placement automatically by default versus letting users/DIRAC decide, but no specific new requirement was settled beyond noting this depends on how the future DIRAC-based scheduling/data-movement component is designed.
- Job overflow: already supported and allowed today (jobs may run away from their data and read remotely via AAA when local resources are unavailable). Users can currently force a job to run only where its data resides, overriding overflow/locality decisions — Stefano Belforte described this as dangerous, historically problematic, and something that, in his opinion, should not exist going forward, though this was not adopted as a formal requirement change in the meeting. Alan Malta Rodrigues expressed inclination toward treating job-overflow support as foundational to the new system for efficient resource use.
- Stefano Belforte's closing recommendation: most SI/production-level requirements (remote access, CMSSW provisioning, arbitrary executables, data movement, job splitting, etc.) are shared between production and user analysis; the few genuinely user-specific additions are (1) multi-user/superuser scoping (not a single-user system), (2) sandboxing to let users provide their own executable environment, and (3) differentiated handling of user output versus production output.

### Submission Infrastructure requirements ("SI discussion," with Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo, Todor)

- Pilot job model: agreed as a preserved/must-have requirement — the future system must preserve the pilot-job model with late binding (or an equivalent providing the same flexibility for resource acquisition and opportunistic usage), with a clear point of contact/coupling between WM and the submission infrastructure (mainly Condor) — Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo characterized this as ensuring the future WM couples gracefully with SI rather than assuming any particular WM internal design.
- Fair share/priorities/quotas across groups: clarified as ambiguous wording — "priority" in Condor has a fairly specific meaning (queue ordering given available resources), whereas "priority" in the broader WM context is overloaded (data placement, physics importance, etc.). Marco Mascheroni explained the underlying intent is to move beyond the current three broad accounting groups (production, analysis, Tier 0) toward finer-grained groups (e.g., per-physics-area subgroups), addressing a gap currently filled ad hoc by tools like McM. Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo pushed back that this finer-grained grouping is a WM-layer concern, not necessarily a Submission Infrastructure requirement — the group agreed this specific bullet should be reframed as a "nice to have"/feature the SI can offer rather than a hard requirement, with Todor noting the SI side can already push quota implementation down to the submission-infrastructure/matchmaking layer if useful (e.g., not matching jobs against sites lacking sufficient disk cache), which in turn implies a job-characterization requirement (jobs declaring needed disk/other resources via class-ad-like attributes) that had not previously been explicitly captured.
- Materializing jobs in schedulers as soon as possible (point 3): substantial, unresolved debate. Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo argued the WM layer should be as transparent as possible and push as much as possible into the queues, letting Condor's matchmaking handle availability, since artificially throttling submission (as today, e.g., sizing pushes to an assumed site core count) works against SI's built-in tendency to expand and acquire more resources, and limits the system's ability to react to sudden, large, possibly short-lived resource availability (e.g., an HPC allocation). Marco Mascheroni and Alan Malta Rodrigues countered that job *diversity* (a mix of core/memory footprints), not sheer job count, is what's actually needed to exploit heterogeneous resources, and that materializing/pausing/resuming/aborting large numbers of jobs has real, non-trivial cost in upper WM layers (database updates, propagation across job class-ads) that differs fundamentally from the near-zero cost pending jobs have for Condor itself. Antonio countered that this cost should instead be treated as something the future system's design should minimize and that the WM-side infrastructure should be scaled to afford, rather than accepted as a structural throttle. No resolution was reached; explicitly identified as a broader CMS-level design trade-off (resource-utilization efficiency vs. workload-prioritization control) requiring wider input, not just an SI/WM implementation detail.
- Dynamic/burst resource availability: Todor proposed generalizing the "burst resource" point as an example of a broader "flexible, dynamic resource management" capability rather than a standalone requirement — agreed.
- Steady pool pressure (point 5): Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo explained this is about minimizing the losses incurred when the pool contracts (draining resources before returning them to the provider) and the related need to periodically drain pilots to defragment slots (e.g., when job-shape demand shifts from 4-core to 8-core jobs); Marco Mascheroni added that with sufficient job diversity, "draining" is not actually necessary — mismatched capacity can instead be backfilled with other job shapes (e.g., analysis jobs) without waiting for a full pilot drain. Stefano Belforte questioned why this should be framed as a WM requirement at all rather than an SI-internal optimization concern (deciding whether to wait or kill idle pilot slots); Stefano's framing: WM's job is to keep the queue well-stocked with diverse, ready-to-run work (including not overloading it with work that won't actually be needed soon, e.g., data pending from tape) — after that, it is SI's job to decide how to use idle capacity.
- Clean boundary between orchestration and scheduling/resource-management layers (point 6): agreed as a genuine requirement (Todor) — WM/DIRAC and the submission-infrastructure/Condor layer should be decoupled enough that each can be scaled independently based on its own relevant parameters (e.g., memory/CPU for WM deployments, vs. Condor's own scaling needs), rather than architectures like one-agent-per-scheduler. Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed phrasing this as decoupling WM from Condor, plus the ability to talk to any Condor pool via a REST interface regardless of location. Todor separately noted (as a "good to have," not pursued further here) a wish for Condor to support more structured, hierarchical resource description (vs. flat classad lists) to make targeting more sophisticated resource subsets easier — flagged as a potential future ask to the Condor developers, not part of this requirements document.
- Job checkpointing and job eviction: confirmed by Todor as an existing must-have requirement already captured elsewhere in the document (item 9/9.9).
- Flexible pilot backfill mechanism (point 7): introduced as a "good to have" (not a hard requirement). Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo explained backfilling means using otherwise-wasted slot fragments during a pilot's draining/retiring phase (e.g., its final ~4 hours) with additional short jobs, improving scheduling efficiency by an estimated few percent, mainly relevant for large whole-node machines rather than small (e.g., 8-core) allocations. The transcript ends while this point is still being discussed.

## Open Questions

- How to phrase output-data-placement requirements without relying on the CMS/CRAB-specific concept of a dataset being "announced," in a way that also makes sense for DIRAC or other systems — flagged as needing rework by Alan Malta Rodrigues; not resolved in the meeting.
- Whether WM should persist its own local record of Rucio rules at all, versus relying on Rucio-side metadata (e.g., an embedded workflow ID) to look up rules on demand — raised by Hasan Ozturk and discussed with Eric Vaandering's workflow-ID-in-metadata idea, but not settled, including the unresolved caveat about Rucio's rule-deduplication behavior potentially hiding multi-workflow reliance on a single rule.
- Whether a many-to-one parent-child Rucio rule relationship (many block-level rules feeding one container-level tape rule) is something Rucio could support in the future, and whether responsibility for rule cleanup logic could be shifted entirely into Rucio (conditional cleanup once a tape rule succeeds) rather than remaining a WM responsibility — raised by Hasan Ozturk as a possible future Rucio feature request, not resolved.
- Whether/how output-data checksum verification requirements should change if CMS moves toward merging output at the destination site rather than the current model — raised by Marco Mascheroni and Eric Vaandering, not resolved.
- Whether checksum/data-integrity verification of files written to storage is feasible uniformly across different storage backends and GFAL utilities — raised by Alan Malta Rodrigues, not resolved.
- Whether CMS will commit to a "transparent" CRAB-compatible migration path or accept a more disruptive DIRAC-native path requiring the community to learn new tooling — actively debated (Stefano Belforte favoring the disruptive/native path, Vijay/document currently favoring transparent) and explicitly flagged as needing higher-level management/community buy-in (Andrea Piccinelli), not decided in this meeting.
- Whether the requirements being drafted should be framed as CMS-specific input into a broader, cross-experiment WM effort, or as general/experiment-agnostic requirements — raised by Marco Mascheroni ahead of the upcoming retreat; Andrea Piccinelli's view was that the requirements should be presented as CMS's input and could spark discussion with other experiments (e.g., ATLAS, LHCb), but this framing question was not fully settled.
- Whether WM should automatically trigger input-data placement/movement for users by default, or let users (or DIRAC's scheduling logic) control this explicitly — raised by Alan Malta Rodrigues and Stefano Belforte, tied to undecided design choices in DIRAC/DiracX's own scheduler, not resolved.
- The central, unresolved design trade-off between maximizing job materialization/throttling-free submission to fully exploit Submission Infrastructure's resource-acquisition capability (Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo's position) versus limiting materialization due to real bookkeeping costs in upper WM layers and prioritizing job diversity over raw volume (Alan Malta Rodrigues/Marco Mascheroni's position) — explicitly identified as needing a wider CMS-level decision.
- Whether/how the flexible pilot backfill mechanism (point 7) should be scoped as a requirement — discussion was in progress when the transcript ends, with Antonio Perez-Calero Yzquierdo already expressing it should be a "good to have" of modest impact, not a priority.
