# New WM Dev Team Weekly Meeting — WMS Evaluation Criteria for Offline Computing Week

## Summary

This session continued the group's ongoing evaluation of candidate future workload-management strategies for CMS — adopting [[DIRAC]], adopting [[PanDA]], or building a CMS-specific solution on top of [[HTCondor]]/[[glideinWMS]] (or continuing to evolve [[WMCore]]) — in preparation for a report to the Offline Computing Week ("O&C Week") on the following Tuesday. The transcript frequently refers to "(the) Dublin Core (repository/review)"; from context (e.g. "the WM Core hat," "one repository, 10 products") this is almost certainly a mis-transcription of "WM Core" and is treated below as referring to [[WMCore]].

The group worked through three prepared slides — codebase/project quality ("Project QA"), monitoring and observability, and CMS-required changes to adopt an external system — refining which sub-items to highlight, without reaching final wording for the presentation. Discussion repeatedly returned to a deeper tension raised mainly by Valentin Y Kuznetsov and Marco Mascheroni: whether adopting a "20-year-old" community system (DIRAC or PanDA) actually serves the technical goal of a system that scales to HL-LHC, versus the financial/sustainability goal of joining an external community, and whether these two goals are even compatible. Andrea Piccinelli reiterated that long-term financial sustainability (over roughly the next 10 years) was the primary driver identified in the prior WMCore review, but was skeptical that funding agencies would support an effort perceived as CMS-only, and noted past attempts to build outside interest in a CMS-led community project have not gained traction. The meeting also surfaced a recurring worry (Marco Mascheroni) that regardless of which technical path is chosen, CMS Comp Ops needs to be more closely integrated into the decision and design process, and an unresolved question (Alan Malta Rodrigues, Valentin Y Kuznetsov) about how honestly versus how diplomatically the group's findings should be presented to management, given that the outcome affects the team's own roles. The transcript ends mid-sentence as Alan Malta Rodrigues proposes to expand further on the "CMS-required changes" slide before drafting a final recommendation.

## Decisions / Conclusions

- No final decision was reached on which WMS strategy (DIRAC, PanDA, or a CMS-built solution) to recommend; this remains an open evaluation.
- The group agreed (informally, via discussion and no objection, including from Vijay) that presence of unit tests and automated CI/CD pipelines should be marked as an important evaluation criterion in the codebase-quality slide.
- Marco Mascheroni proposed, and the group did not dispute, that if CMS adopts a "thin integration" strategy that only reuses a small fraction of an external system's stack (e.g. only PanDA server and client), while re-implementing matchmaking/pilot submission separately (as glideinWMS + HTCondor already effectively does), the result risks being an overly complex, duplicated ("monster") architecture; this was raised as a reason to be explicit, in the report, about the trade-off between full-solution adoption and thin integration.
- The group leaned toward stating the codebase-quality/complexity findings as qualitative (low/medium/high) ratings rather than quantified FTE or time estimates, since the underlying investigation cannot produce reliable numeric cost estimates (Kenyi Hurtado Anampa, supported by Alan Malta Rodrigues and Marco Mascheroni).
- Alan Malta Rodrigues and Valentin Y Kuznetsov agreed the presentation should note that neither DIRAC nor PanDA can simply be "taken and put on a bare VM" — both require substantial configuration expertise to deploy and operate correctly.

## Discussion

### Evaluation criteria: software development lifecycle and codebase quality
- Alan Malta Rodrigues outlined candidate sub-items for the "Project QA" slide: software development lifecycle/release cadence, code-stack organization (repository structure, separation of concerns, monolithic vs. modular design), contribution guidelines, lines of code, documentation quality, presence of unit tests/CI/CD pipelines, use of AI-assisted development, and community/stakeholder engagement (e.g. DIRAC's user "retreats"/hackathons).
- In response to a question from Duong Nguyen about whether DIRAC/PanDA would make development easier than WMCore, Marco Mascheroni noted that the difficulty of evolving WMCore and adding new features was one of the main motivations for last year's review that triggered this whole evaluation.
- Debate between Alan Malta Rodrigues and Marco Mascheroni on integration philosophy: Alan argued CMS should try to reuse/recycle as much of an adopted system as possible to maximize shared community effort and long-term sustainability. Marco pushed back that this philosophy, taken to the extreme (e.g. keeping PanDA's own matchmaking and pilot submission on top of glideinWMS/HTCondor, which do matchmaking themselves), produces redundant, layered complexity ("a monster") worse than the current system. Alan clarified he did not mean stacking things on top of the adopted system, but recycling/reusing as much as feasible without unnecessary duplication; both agreed the trade-off depends heavily on available effort (FTEs).
- Antonio asked whether CMS would share a single codebase/repository with the adopting community or fork and diverge, and how merge decisions would be made with other stakeholders; this was acknowledged as a key open question without a settled answer, tied to how much of the community's process CMS would need to follow.
- Antonio suggested the Offline Computing Week presentation should include concrete cost ("price tag") estimates for each option, not only technical descriptions, so that people can make an informed choice ("green light/red light") rather than assuming a decision has effectively already been made. Alan Malta Rodrigues and Kenyi Hurtado Anampa responded that only rough, qualitative estimates (e.g. low/medium/high) are realistic at this stage, since real costs and blockers typically only surface once implementation actually begins; Kenyi noted the investigation's original goal was feasibility, not a detailed implementation plan.
- Kenyi Hurtado Anampa proposed splitting cost/complexity metrics by time horizon: short-term (initial development effort), mid-term (maintainability effort, including "split-brain"/two-engines risk from running duplicate matchmaking or brokerage logic in parallel — assessed as higher risk for the PanDA "keep everything" strategy and lower for other strategies), and long-term (architectural agility/evolution needs, e.g. adapting to new GPU requirements or database schema changes). Marco Mascheroni added "extendability"/architectural simplicity as a related concern.
- Code-stack organization: Alan Malta Rodrigues reiterated that WMCore's single-repository, multi-product structure is a known "big red flag" that makes it hard to reason about the blast radius of a change. Documentation for both PanDA and DIRAC was assessed as generally good and easy to navigate, aside from deployment documentation.
- Kenyi Hurtado Anampa raised "coupling of services" — how many components/layers a given feature change must touch — as an important but hard-to-measure complexity dimension not captured by lines-of-code counts; Alan Malta Rodrigues agreed this is difficult to assess from outside a system, requiring deep familiarity to answer reliably (true of WMCore as well).
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov described his own ongoing difficulty setting up and understanding DIRAC despite ~40 minutes of joint troubleshooting with Federica, illustrating that any adopted community system will likely require extensive training (he estimated potentially up to a year to reach proficiency), because the complexity is inherent to the system's accumulated history, not specific to DIRAC.
- Lines of code as a complexity metric: Valentin Y Kuznetsov argued lines of code is a very good proxy for complexity and the difficulty of acquiring system knowledge. Rough figures mentioned by Alan Malta Rodrigues (uncertain/approximate, read off a document, transcription unclear) were roughly on the order of a few hundred thousand lines for WMCore and PanDA, with the group concluding all three systems are of a broadly similar order of magnitude, and Marco Mascheroni noting that DIRAC and PanDA together perform roughly what glideinWMS + HTCondor perform for CMS.
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov separately observed that true modularity (being able to swap one component for another via a known, schema-based protocol) is undermined in practice by Python's dynamic-typing style of passing untyped dictionaries between components — a pattern he said applies across DIRAC, PanDA, and WMCore alike, not a criticism specific to one system.
- Deployment: PanDA's production deployment runs on VM nodes, via a Python environment (uncertain whether from PyPI or built from the repository); PanDA also provides Helm charts, used for the ATLAS testbed and a multi-VO "PanDA/DOMA" instance on Kubernetes. Both Alan Malta Rodrigues and Valentin Y Kuznetsov described PanDA's Helm-based deployment as having many configuration hooks/dependencies and poor documentation ("a nightmare"). Valentin Y Kuznetsov reported similar difficulty configuring DIRAC correctly even after installing its components, requiring expert help. Both agreed the presentation should state plainly that deployment for either system requires significant configuration tuning, not a "drop-in and run" experience.
- Repository/community statistics: Valentin Y Kuznetsov proposed adding total open-ticket counts as a metric, referencing WMCore's historical backlog (on the order of a few hundred tickets) as something that visibly slows development; Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed broadening this to general "repository statistics" (releases/tags cadence, number of contributors via GitHub insights). Alan noted this is complicated by some projects (e.g. PanDA) spanning roughly ten repositories with uneven test/CI coverage across them.
- Testing/sandboxing: Valentin Y Kuznetsov raised the lack of a sandbox environment for testing individual DIRAC components (e.g. a database or web server change) without deploying the entire agent stack as a significant source of development friction, distinct from raw code complexity. Alan Malta Rodrigues agreed this is related to, but more specific than, the software-development-lifecycle and deployment-environment items already on the slide, and suggested adding an explicit sandbox/isolated-testing capability sub-item.

### Monitoring and observability
- Candidate sub-items discussed: monitoring technologies used, generic vs. experiment-specific monitoring, accessibility (web portals/APIs), extensibility via plugins for experiment-specific metrics, tracing/observability support, data aggregation and filtering, near-real-time (roughly 1–5 minute latency, not sub-second) workflow and WMS-level monitoring, ETA estimation for job/request completion, and availability of job logs.
- Marco Mascheroni suggested also capturing whether a system offloads monitoring to externally maintained infrastructure/teams (reducing CMS's own maintenance burden), and separately raised whether grid/site-infrastructure monitoring (site/queue availability, diagnosing broken sites) should be in scope, given the earlier discussion of possibly adopting PanDA's or DIRAC's own workload engine without full glideinWMS integration; this was left open, pending clarity on what message the group wants to deliver to level-1 management.
- Alan Malta Rodrigues noted CMS's current resource monitoring (e.g. via CRAB) is not an authoritative data source in itself — it is built on top of dashboards originally created by the site-support team (Hammer Cloud, SAM tests) — so it is unclear whether adopting DIRAC or PanDA would mean keeping that resource monitoring and only adding WMS-level monitoring on top, or replacing it.
- DIRAC's monitoring was reported (Alan Malta Rodrigues) to rely partly on OpenSearch; Valentin Y Kuznetsov clarified OpenSearch is primarily used as a non-relational document store for job descriptions (for querying/searching), and it was unclear to him how much of DIRAC's graphing/visualization monitoring actually depends on it.
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov argued strongly for industry-standard monitoring stacks (e.g. [[Prometheus]] + [[Grafana]]) over custom-built monitoring UIs (citing PanDA's monitoring as an example he dislikes), because standard tooling makes adding new metrics/dashboards comparatively trivial, whereas extending a custom product requires development inside that product.
- Extended debate on "availability of job logs": Valentin Y Kuznetsov cautioned that simply stating logs are "available" is misleading and potentially "dangerous" without specifying how a typical user would need to navigate to reach them (e.g. CMS logs today require logging into an agent node); he argued true availability requires routing through centrally available services with common, standardized interfaces/APIs, not experiment- or role-specific access paths. Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed rephrasing the criterion as "human-friendly" availability of job logs — e.g. WMStats-style click-to-view logs (which he noted PanDA does reasonably well) versus more convoluted paths requiring direct node access, EOS, or XRootD.
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov connected this to OpenTelemetry as the industry-standard approach that would make such availability effectively "free" if adopted from the start, but noted retrofitting it into an existing system (WMCore, DIRAC, or PanDA) is a very large undertaking requiring changes across many components. He noted DiracX has begun adding telemetry/observability, but so far only at the web-service (HTTP request tracing) level, not for job/pilot execution on compute elements.
- Marco Mascheroni raised a specific concern (which he flagged as recurring for him) that PanDA's current monitoring UI, examined in the context of a potential glideinWMS integration, is confusing to interpret from a CMS perspective (unclear what constitutes a "pilot" vs. a final job vs. a test, and no visibility into HTCondor logs in the CMS global pool); he assessed that such a UI would likely need to be substantially rebuilt to make sense in a CMS/glideinWMS-integrated deployment.
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov noted that assessing whether either system's monitoring will hold up at CMS/HL-LHC scale is inherently difficult to predict, especially for DIRAC (not currently operating at CMS's scale) and, to a lesser but still real degree, for PanDA.
- The group tentatively marked "monitoring technologies adopted" as a high-importance item to expand on (Marco Mascheroni), along with ETA estimation, which Marco noted has specifically been requested by Stefan (surname uncertain in the transcript, referenced as "Stefan Lamel"/possibly Stefan Piperov).

### CMS-required ("acquired") changes and adoption strategy
- Alan Malta Rodrigues framed this slide around the roadmaps of the external systems (DIRAC moving toward [[DIRACX]]; PanDA reportedly considering integrating iDDS features into the PanDA server) and their implications for CMS's own required changes, plus CMS-specific requirements the adopted system must meet (as currently assumed: glideinWMS integration, and continuing to support Tier-0 operations and analysis tooling).
- Discussed sub-items: integration model with existing CMS-specific services/stakeholders (PPD, unified, [[DBS]], [[Rucio]]); potential cultural shifts in how CMS operates (e.g. moving from splitting files across multiple jobs to processing whole files, as an illustrative example, not a decision); database schema changes affecting DBS/DAS (Andrea Piccinelli flagged uncertainty about exact scope, e.g. possible Lumi-section-related changes); and whether CMS would keep DBS as the authoritative data-bookkeeping system or adopt a chosen system's native bookkeeping — Alan Malta Rodrigues noted DIRAC's internal bookkeeping is oriented toward the workload itself rather than collaboration-wide bookkeeping the way DBS is.
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov framed the integration choice as two extremes: (1) minimize ownership cost by adopting the external system's way of doing things as-is, accepting large process/cultural impact on CMS; or (2) build CMS-specific behavior on top of the external system's core (as glideinWMS does today), which reduces cultural impact but increases development and long-term maintenance complexity. He noted a CMS-built-from-scratch solution avoids this dilemma entirely (no need to change DBS or existing submission/monitoring workflows) but forgoes any shared "community" benefit, and it is not obvious in advance which route ends up costing more overall.
- Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed a bare-minimum baseline of required changes regardless of which route is chosen: the adopted system must support data discovery via DBS and injection of newly produced data into DBS.
- Duong Nguyen asked for a concrete example (e.g. what would need to change in DBS if PanDA were installed at CMS next month) to make the scale of adaptation tangible for an Offline Computing Week audience. Valentin Y Kuznetsov responded that there is no single such example — adopting either system would require changes or adaptation at nearly every layer, which is itself the core finding, and that in his personal assessment building a system from scratch could plausibly be less time- and resource-consuming than adapting DIRAC or PanDA, since DIRAC, PanDA, and WMCore are "alike" in the sense that all three would face broadly similar long-term maintenance/support burdens.
- Marco Mascheroni noted that building a solution from scratch is a more realistic option now than even six months to a year ago, citing his own recent experience using AI coding agents to accelerate development.
- Andrea Piccinelli countered that no funding-agency support is likely for an effort perceived as CMS-only; convincing funders requires a credible plan to build genuine external community interest, and past attempts by CMS to generate outside interest in a CMS-led community project (e.g. around HTCondor-adjacent development) have not gained traction — noting HTCondor itself has by far the largest existing community of the systems discussed, but CMS currently only consumes it rather than contributing to it. He was also skeptical that Comp Ops is currently willing to co-develop a new system over a multi-year timeframe rather than wanting something that "just works," and suggested this dynamic could reflect a management-level issue rather than a technical one.
- Marco Mascheroni raised a recurring, cross-cutting concern: regardless of which technical path is chosen (PanDA, DIRAC, or a CMS-built solution), the WM dev team needs to work much more closely and continuously with Comp Ops as a single team throughout development, rather than designing something in isolation over an extended period and then handing it off.
- Alan Malta Rodrigues raised, as a related but distinct open point, uncertainty about the right timing to broadly engage Comp Ops and other stakeholders — now, while the direction is still undecided, versus later once a direction is chosen — noting in hindsight that Vijay and Antonio arguably should have joined the investigation earlier.
- On generality: Alan Malta Rodrigues argued that even a CMS-led solution should aim to be reasonably generic/reusable by other experiments/communities rather than CMS-specific, in order to build external community value, but noted a tension with guidance he attributed to conversations with Andrea Piccinelli and Kevin Lannon, that ideally partner communities should be identified before such a system is built, not after.
- On HTCondor's role: Alan Malta Rodrigues questioned how much CMS contributing requirements/features directly to HTCondor would actually grow "CMS's community," since HTCondor is only one layer in the stack. Marco Mascheroni agreed CMS should instead build a workflow-and-data-management layer on top of Condor and aim to make that layer reusable/attractive to other communities, rather than CMS becoming HTCondor core contributors. Alan Malta Rodrigues noted that even after offloading as much as possible to HTCondor/glideinWMS, substantial orchestration work remains above that layer (DAG/workflow entry handling, job splitting and data discovery, result aggregation, retry logic).
- Marco Mascheroni proposed the team build an explicit comparison table across the candidate strategies (PanDA, DIRAC, from-scratch), scoring dimensions such as upfront development effort, long-term maintainability cost, and potential to build/join a community — and stressed the team should first agree internally on this framing before "corridor talks" at Offline Computing Week, to avoid presenting inconsistent positions to different people.

### Presentation approach and framing
- Valentin Y Kuznetsov raised a tension between presenting an objective/honest technical assessment (potentially concluding that CMS would not clearly be better off under either adopted system) versus a more diplomatically framed presentation, given his impression that the underlying decision is largely financially driven and may already be effectively predetermined, and that the team is being asked to present findings rather than make the decision. He noted this is a difficult position because the outcome directly affects the team members' own roles.
- Alan Malta Rodrigues disagreed that the team has no real influence, arguing that as the domain experts operating these systems daily, the team's recommendations should carry substantial (if informal) weight — unless "broadening the community" is weighted so heavily in management's decision criteria that other technical factors become largely irrelevant, in which case the outcome would already be effectively determined. Marco Mascheroni said he has no visibility into how heavily community-broadening actually factors into the decision.
- Andrea Piccinelli suggested first agreeing internally on the pros and cons of each option before deciding how to frame the recommendation.

## Open Questions

- Whether/how to quantify cost or effort differences between the strategies for the Offline Computing Week presentation, given the group's view that only rough qualitative (low/medium/high) estimates are currently defensible.
- How to meaningfully measure "coupling of services"/cross-layer complexity for a candidate feature change — no concrete metric was identified.
- Whether Comp Ops (or another team) should be engaged now, while the strategic direction is still open, or later once a direction is chosen.
- Whether a CMS-built, from-scratch solution could realistically attract external community interest and funding-agency support — Andrea Piccinelli was skeptical; unresolved.
- Whether the goals of (a) a system that scales for HL-LHC and (b) long-term financial sustainability via joining an external community are actually compatible — raised as an open tension by Valentin Y Kuznetsov, not resolved.
- Whether/how much CMS's current resource monitoring (built on CRAB/site-support dashboards) would be retained or replaced under DIRAC or PanDA adoption.
- Whether DBS should remain CMS's authoritative data-bookkeeping system or be replaced by an adopted system's native bookkeeping.
- How much weight "broadening the community" carries in the eventual management decision — unknown to the team as of this meeting.
- Whether the group should present an objective/critical assessment or a more diplomatically framed one to management — raised by Valentin Y Kuznetsov, unresolved.
- The exact numeric lines-of-code figures cited for PanDA and WMCore during the meeting were not clearly captured in the transcript and should be treated as approximate/uncertain.
