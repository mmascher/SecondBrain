# Planning WM Retreat and DIRAC Team Session

## Summary

Marco Mascheroni and Alan Malta Rodrigues discussed how to organize two related in-person gatherings during an upcoming event (referred to as involving a shared agenda with other sessions, "next week" relative to the meeting date): an internal WM team retreat, and a possible separate session involving the DIRAC/DiracX team. The conversation was triggered by an email from Stefan, which Marco felt was based on a misunderstanding — Stefan had replied suggesting it would be a good idea to use the opportunity to meet the DIRAC team in person, when Marco's original intent had been an internal WM retreat.

Alan expressed reservations about involving the DIRAC team too heavily next week, given they are currently focused on finishing their ADRs (Architectural Design Reviews), which were expected "by tomorrow" but might slip; he preferred to keep deeper DIRAC-team engagement for the later DIRAC workshop, when more people would be present. Marco proposed a tentative split instead: an internal retreat on Tuesday (with Antonio and Francesco Brivio also present), and, if the ADRs are ready and digested by then, a session with the DIRAC team on Thursday to discuss them. They agreed the right approach was for Marco to reach out informally/privately to Federico to gauge DIRAC-team interest, rather than issuing a more formal or public invitation that could feel hard to decline.

Marco explained the underlying objective of engaging the DIRAC team: the Submission Infrastructure (SI) layer has historically been treated as a "black box" by others, and the goal is for WM team members to understand it in more depth so they can better propose ideas and inform the design of the new system. Federico's talk on Monday was noted as another opportunity to exchange ideas on this. Alan proposed conceptually splitting future engagement into two kinds of sessions: a broader "onboarding" session educating people across different CMS groups about the SI stack, versus a more narrowly focused discussion specifically about the DiracX/HL-LHC direction for CMS. He also suggested using a prior document (referred to as "Kingji's" or "Kingshi's" document — the name was not consistently transcribed) as a baseline for the discussion, even without the ADRs, and incorporating ADR insights live if they become available.

The rest of the discussion covered logistics: checking the agenda for conflicting sessions on Tuesday morning (a software-roadmap talk, and a talk — name transcribed inconsistently as "Gilemmo"/"Guilamu" — on AI/ML applied to detector simulation, which Alan thought might be relevant to the workload management system via Monte Carlo), the risk of morning sessions overrunning into the afternoon, room booking (possibly in Building 40), and the fact that Antonio and Francesco Brivio are traveling on Wednesday. Alan noted a personal scheduling conflict (a "request manager" commitment on Sunday morning, and a possible preference to attend the AI/ML talk instead of the internal Tuesday session) and suggested a narrower window (e.g., 4–6pm) could work instead of a full afternoon. Whether to publicly advertise/add either session to the agenda was discussed but not clearly resolved (see Open Questions).
