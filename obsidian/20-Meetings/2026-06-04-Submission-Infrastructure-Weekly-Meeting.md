# Submission Infrastructure Weekly Meeting

## Summary

Marco opened with an update on a Tier 0 / Tier 1 resource-quota problem: because Tier 0 jobs include Fermilab in their desired-site list, and Fermilab was assigned to the Tier 1 negotiator, Fermilab could never reach the 60% Tier 1 quota that had been reserved for Tier 0 jobs. Marco's fix was to move Fermilab out of the Tier 1 negotiator and into what was the US Tier 2 negotiator (now referred to as the US negotiator, since it includes Fermilab as a Tier 1), and to raise the Tier 1 negotiator's quota from 60% to 80% of Tier 1 resources (now excluding Fermilab) to keep the same amount of resources available to Tier 0. Marco reported this change is already in place and working. Luis asked whether this produced a visible improvement in the Tier 0 backlog, but Marco explained he does not normally monitor that dashboard; he described the backlog as being about input data files accumulating ahead of the Tier 0 repack/prompt-reconstruction chain (via the HLT "Store Manager" system) rather than about idle jobs, since Tier 0 releases jobs into the queue gradually rather than all at once. Both agreed Antonio would need to be asked about that dashboard.

Luis raised a factory-operations ticket involving a Tier 2 (Florida) CE with a hardware problem that the site wants disabled, prompting a discussion of how site downtime is handled. Luis proposed recommending the site admin declare downtime through the standard topology/GOCDB mechanism (so the factory auto-disables the entry, and the site can self re-enable once fixed) rather than the team manually disabling the entry. This led to a broader discussion of the `auto_downtimes.sh`-based cron mechanism (an "op factory show auto downtimes" script) that reads site downtime status from GOCDB (EGI sites) or OSG Topology (OSG/US sites) and puts factory entries into downtime automatically. Hyunwoo checked the Fermilab factory and found no such cron job configured; the CERN factory does have it, and Luis confirmed CERN factory entries have previously been auto-disabled this way when sites declared downtime in topology. The status of the OSG factory (run by Jeff) was unclear and needs to be cross-checked with Jeff. The group agreed factories should ideally behave consistently, and agreed to eventually enable this mechanism on the Fermilab factory (Luis and Hyunwoo to take time to work on it, and to discuss with Jeff). In the meantime, for the immediate Florida Tier 2 ticket, Luis said he would just disable the entry manually at CERN and let Marco know once done.

Hyunwoo gave an update on his plan (first reported two weeks earlier) to prevent user jobs from CRAB/CMS Connect from being scheduled at Fermilab Tier 1 in the event of a critical Linux issue there. Marco explained the negotiator has a "slot constraint" (used to partition resources between the three negotiators — Tier 1, US, and a third) and a separate, currently-unused "job constraint" that could instead be used to exclude specific jobs from matching. Working through the syntax together, they converged on excluding (via the Tier 1 negotiator's job constraint) jobs where `Owner` indicates an analysis job (as opposed to production) AND the job's desired-site list includes Fermilab, using `stringListMember` (since desired sites is a list, not a scalar equality). Marco cautioned this would remove such jobs from matching on all Tier 1s, not just Fermilab, so an analysis job that could also run at another Tier 1 would be blocked there too — a tradeoff Hyunwoo acknowledged. Hyunwoo agreed to work out the exact syntax and test it on the ITB cluster, and separately plans to fail the negotiator over from its normal host to a Fermilab backup collector to do this testing without needing to touch Puppet-managed configuration (Marco noted he similarly avoids touching Puppet in ITB and instead edits local `99-local` files directly, given Florian's current unavailability).

Luis and Hyunwoo discussed the ongoing change (previously approved in principle by Jeff, pending Marco's confirmation) to move HTCondor-CE entries from site-token verification to site tokens, currently deployed only in the ITB factory. Marco confirmed he was fine with the change (already discussed earlier with Luis) and said he would message Jeff on Slack to confirm. Hyunwoo noted this is also something that needs to be adopted on the Fermilab factory. Luis said he plans to announce the change on CMS Talk on Monday, after applying it to the CERN production factory, deliberately avoiding a Friday rollout.

Luis raised that two site-admin tickets he opened about a week earlier have had no response, and asked how to escalate. Marco noted this is a recurring open question — there is no clear escalation path when a site admin doesn't respond — and that for opportunistic sites like Syracuse (part of the ticket in question) there is no real leverage; the normal US Tier 1 escalation contact (formerly Andrew Melo) doesn't apply here. Marco suggested finding the current site admin's identity via OSG Topology and emailing them directly, and said he would ask around (e.g., at HTCondor Week the following week, where he and Jeff will both be) whether anyone knows who currently administers Syracuse. For the Fermilab-side ticket, Luis noted it's less of a concern since it's a local site, and observed (via GitOps/ticket viewers) that some Fermilab staff had already looked at the ticket even without a reply.

A related question came up about a separate ticket: the site token used for global-pool pilots was being rejected by a Fermilab CE (identified as the LPC CE / CE2, `cms` local entry) during `condor_ping` testing. Marco and Hyunwoo clarified the mechanism: this CE is configured (via a `CMS_LOCAL_ENTRY = true` attribute) to only accept global-pool pilots whose jobs belong to LPC-local users, via a separate credential/group used by the global-pool frontend specifically for this case. Hyunwoo confirmed this behavior is expected, and the group concluded there is no actual problem — the CE correctly does not accept the normal (non-local) site token. Luis said he would close/update the ticket accordingly. Separately, Luis noted Syracuse remains the only site still rejecting the standard site token, and is also known to have been rejecting proxies for some entries for a while; he has taken note of the latter but not yet opened tickets for it, planning to follow up later since there are several such cases. For next steps, Marco suggested mentioning ongoing work on reducing noisy/false alerts (following a recent incident-related discussion) and proposed a new idea: a single unified dashboard correlating pilot efficiency, scheduling efficiency, and payload efficiency (potentially in collaboration with PNR, Gregor, or Hassan), to address the current fragmentation across separate monitoring views. Marco also said the talk was missing a clear "headline" slide demonstrating, with the new monitoring, that overloading measurably improves efficiency (e.g., a plus-~10% efficiency plot), suggesting a Fermilab-specific example given Fermilab's whole-node scheduling model. Toward the end, Luis mentioned HTCondor Week (referred to as possibly the "HTCondor European Workshop") is now scheduled in Lyon, approximately September 29–October 2, 2026, which overlaps with the CERN School of Computing that Luis had also wanted to attend. ## Discussion

### Tier 0 / Tier 1 negotiator quota restructuring
Marco explained that reserving 60% of Tier 1 quota for Tier 0 jobs didn't work because Tier 0 jobs list Fermilab in their desired sites, and Fermilab was assigned to the Tier 1 negotiator — meaning Fermilab itself could never contribute toward that 60% figure. His fix moved Fermilab into the negotiator previously used only for US Tier 2s (now called the US negotiator, since it includes a Tier 1), and raised the Tier 1 negotiator's quota to 80% of Tier 1 resources excluding Fermilab, preserving the overall Tier 0 resource share. He reported this is already deployed and working.

### Tier 0 backlog
Luis asked whether the quota change improved the Tier 0 backlog, but said he isn't confident reading the relevant Grafana dashboard. Marco clarified the backlog in question is about accumulating input data files ahead of Tier 0 processing (the HLT "Store Manager" system feeding single-core repack jobs, which run only at CERN with the highest priority, followed by prompt reconstruction producing AOD), constrained by finite buffer space — not about idle jobs, since Tier 0 releases jobs into the queue gradually rather than all at once. Both agreed this needs to be asked of Antonio.

### Florida Tier 2 CE downtime and the auto-downtime mechanism
A Tier 2 site (Florida) has a hardware problem on its CE and wants it disabled. Luis proposed the more sustainable path — the site admin declaring downtime via GOCDB/OSG Topology so the CERN factory's `auto_downtimes.sh` cron mechanism disables the entry automatically (and the site can re-enable it themselves later) — rather than the team manually toggling the entry. Investigating the mechanism, Marco and Luis discussed that the underlying Python script (invoked from a bash cron job) checks GOCDB for EGI sites and OSG Topology (a separate GitHub-based information system, distinct from CMS's own "Creek"/topology-like web interface for CMS site admins) for OSG/US sites. Hyunwoo checked and found no such cron job configured on the Fermilab factory (checked under both the factory user and root); the CERN factory does run it, and CERN entries have previously been auto-disabled this way. It remains to be confirmed whether the OSG factory (Jeff) uses this mechanism. The group agreed factories should behave consistently and to work on enabling this at Fermilab, but for the immediate Florida ticket, Luis said he would simply disable the entry manually at CERN.

### Negotiator job constraint to exclude Fermilab from analysis jobs
Following up on a plan first reported two weeks earlier, Hyunwoo wants to prevent CRAB/CMS Connect user (analysis) jobs from matching Fermilab Tier 1 in the event of a critical Linux issue, without needing to change resource-side (slot) partitioning. Marco explained the Tier 1 negotiator currently uses a slot constraint (for partitioning resources among the three negotiators) but has an empty, unused job constraint that could instead be used to filter jobs. They worked toward excluding jobs where `Owner` indicates an analysis job and the desired-site list contains Fermilab (using `stringListMember` rather than equality, since desired sites is a list), applied as a negative/negated condition on the job constraint (not the slot constraint), since the intent is to exclude specific jobs from matching rather than to make the machine unavailable to all jobs. Marco noted the tradeoff that this would also block matching for the same class of jobs against other Tier 1s where they might otherwise be eligible to run. Hyunwoo will work out the exact syntax and test it on the ITB cluster, and separately plans to test using a negotiator failover to a Fermilab backup collector, to make changes without touching Puppet-managed configuration (paralleling Marco's own practice of editing local `99-local` files directly in ITB).

### Site-token migration
Luis and Hyunwoo discussed the ongoing move of HTCondor-CE entries from grid-proxy-based to site-token authentication, currently active only in ITB, previously discussed with and approved in principle by Jeff pending Marco's confirmation. Marco confirmed he is fine with the change and said he would message Jeff on Slack right away. Hyunwoo noted this also needs to be adopted at Fermilab. Luis plans to apply the change to the CERN production factory and announce it via CMS Talk on Monday, intentionally avoiding a Friday change.

### Unresponsive site-admin tickets

Marco suggested identifying the current site admin via OSG Topology and emailing them directly, since it's unclear whether ticket notifications ("Jigas"/ticketing system) are reaching the right person, and said he would ask around (including at HTCondor Week) about who currently administers Syracuse. For the Fermilab-side ticket, Luis noted lower concern since it's a local site, and confirmed via the ticketing system that some Fermilab staff had already viewed the ticket despite no reply.

### Fermilab LPC-CE global-pool token investigation

Marco and Hyunwoo explained this CE is configured with `CMS_LOCAL_ENTRY = true` and is intended to accept global-pool pilots only when the job owner is an LPC-local user, via a distinct credential/group scope used by a dedicated part of the global-pool frontend. Hyunwoo confirmed this is expected behavior. The group concluded there is no real problem — this CE correctly rejects the normal (non-local) site token — and Luis will update/close the ticket.

### Syracuse token/proxy rejection and core-count follow-up
Syracuse remains the only site still rejecting the standard site token; separately, Luis noted (but hasn't yet ticketed) that Syracuse has also been rejecting proxies on some entries for a while, along with a handful of similar cases elsewhere, planning to address these later. While checking Syracuse in the factory monitoring, Marco and Luis noted the site currently shows around 7,000 running cores, fluctuating over recent weeks (up to ~10K at times), down from a historically larger allocation; both agreed this was worth following up on.

### CHEP presentation review (pilot overloading and efficiency monitoring)
Luis walked Marco and Hyunwoo through an early draft of his CHEP talk. Planned structure: context on efficiency terminology, pilot overloading as the main use case, two improvements (percentage-based per-entry overloading control and new pilot-efficiency monitoring), and next steps. Key points from the discussion:
- **Efficiency terminology**: the group lacks a shared definition of "efficiency" in CMS O&C; Luis proposed defining payload efficiency (how well a payload uses its requested resources, affected by user/CRAB code and by CMSSW task-chain vs. step-chain job splitting), scheduling efficiency (how well payloads are matched to slots — currently ~95%, affected by slot size, payload request size, and pilot retire time), and pilot efficiency (how well a pilot's site-provided resources are used overall, roughly the product of payload and scheduling efficiency, plus glideinWMS startup/cleanup overhead).
- **New monitoring capability**: per-job overloading status is now visible (via MONIT/Condor data), letting users filter specifically for jobs run on non-overloaded pilots — noted as new and worth explicitly advertising.
- **Known gap**: a memory-efficiency plot shows an apparently erroneous value (~18 bytes for a full pilot that ran many jobs, possibly a Naveen/worker-node reporting quirk); Luis intends to keep this in the talk as a lead-in to known monitoring gaps rather than remove it.
- **Next steps discussed**: continuing to improve data quality/availability; improving alerting to reduce noisy alerts (tied to a recent incident-related discussion); and a new idea — a single dashboard correlating pilot, scheduling, and payload efficiency (potentially involving PNR, Gregor, or Hassan) to address current monitoring fragmentation.
- **Missing content**: Marco felt the draft lacked a clear result slide demonstrating overloading's measured efficiency benefit (e.g., a plus-~10% plot), suggesting a Fermilab-specific example given its whole-node scheduling model.
- **Scope/pacing**: both agreed the current draft has too much material for a 20-minute slot; background sections on efficiency and overloading (already presented at past events) should be covered quickly, leaving more time for results.
