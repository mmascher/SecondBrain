# New WM Dev Team Weekly Meeting: Effort Roundtable and Use-Case Boundaries

## Summary

This was day 1 of a two-part session (day 2 planned for the following Wednesday, with no session on the intervening Tuesday). Andrea Piccinelli framed the goal as identifying, for the [[DIRACX]]/[[DIRAC]] transformation and production system work, where the team needs to prioritize effort, and getting concrete conclusions per topic rather than open-ended discussion. The meeting had two main parts: (1) a roundtable in which each participant described their current and expected effort commitment to DIRAC/DiracX development, and a discussion of how bottom-up (developer-picked) vs. top-down (CMS-management-directed) prioritization should work; and (2) a walkthrough of CMS-external-system interaction points organized by use case — Tier-0, Monte Carlo production, and (briefly, before time ran out) user analysis. The meeting was cut short of its planned agenda; the group did not reach point 3 in full and agreed to continue on day 2. Liz Sexton-Kennedy left partway through (around 17:17) due to another commitment, and Alan Malta Rodrigues chaired the remainder.

## Decisions / Conclusions

- The group agreed to continue the unfinished agenda (remainder of the user-analysis discussion, and topics not yet reached) on day 2 (Wednesday), since the meeting was closed at the agreed end time (6pm Eastern) before the agenda was completed.
- Effort/availability stated by participants for DIRAC/DiracX development (as of this meeting, described by several as likely to change over the coming months):
  - Alan Malta Rodrigues: currently ~10% (sprint that started "last Thursday"), planning to ramp up to 20–40% as CMS-side WM-related documentation/requirements work winds down; does not expect to commit 100% even long-term (estimated ceiling ~80%, with 20% remaining on CMS-specific WM work).
  - Liz Sexton-Kennedy: mainly focused on management/coordination rather than direct development.
  - Francesco Brivio: 0% for the current sprint (just starting to ramp up, installing/exploring DiracX); expects ~10–20% starting in August after his holiday, potentially growing to 30–40% once the ADR is released and glideinWMS integration work becomes concrete. His interest is in CMS-side integration between DiracX and glideinWMS.
  - Marco Mascheroni: committing ~20% now, possibly 30–40% later, but noted actual time worked often exceeds committed time. His overall effort is split 25% Submission Infrastructure Level 2, 50% Submission Infrastructure operations, 25% CMS WMS development (of which the DIRAC/DiracX commitment is a subset); noted the team is also short-staffed while hiring Florian.
  - Todor: aiming for an eventual roughly equal 33/33/33 split between DIRAC core development, CMS integration work, and DBS/DAS maintenance/development, but staying conservative at 10–20% for DIRAC for the next few months while USCMS decides. Currently his time is split 50/50 between DBS/DAS and other (WM-related) CMS work; he thinks the 33/33/33 framing needs management sign-off.
  - Valentin Kuznetsov: effort is allocated by US CMS management and currently restricted to WM(S) development (following past guidance to concentrate there); he described this as making it hard to state a target percentage independent of management. His stated personal interest, in priority order, is glideinWMS/HTCondor integration, then the transformation system, and more broadly contributing DB/data-layer expertise (including to DBS ahead of the next LHC run) and pushing for standardized/structured data representations (as opposed to the current reliance on Python dictionaries) across the workflow system, plus OpenTelemetry-based monitoring instrumentation. Currently 50% CMS / 50% Cornell (Peter Wittich is his direct supervisor there); this Cornell split is renegotiated around November via the annual statement of work.
  - Vijay Chakravarty: 100% allocation to WMS (approved by Stefano), though still doing some CRAB work in the near term since CRAB will be handled by "Britton" going forward; open to working on areas beyond CRAB-functionality migration.
  - Kevin Lannon: no development-activity commitment beyond some SI-level coordination.
  - Andrea Piccinelli: realistically ~10% (exceptionally 20%) for development himself, with the rest focused on coordination, particularly leveraging existing relationships with the DIRAC community.
  - Jen Adelman-McCarthy (ops team): not yet committing effort; ops team will be short-handed once Hassan Ahmed's contract ends at the end of July (he has been doing significant front-end/chatbot development work); the ops team's future DIRAC/DiracX involvement is still to be determined.
  - Camille (PhD student, not present) was discussed as a distinct category of resource: expected to spend roughly 30–40% of her time on physics-facing WMS interfaces, with the split against her PhD analysis work expected to shift over the year per Liz Sexton-Kennedy.
- On prioritization process: Andrea Piccinelli described DIRAC's current process as primarily bottom-up (developers pick backlog issues at sprint start, assignment only happens if effort remains unclaimed), with top-down assignment as a fallback. Liz Sexton-Kennedy argued that with two major experiments in the community (one, CMS, needing to catch up so its needs fit the new system), backlog prioritization cannot be left purely to bottom-up developer choice and needs a negotiated, jointly-managed process with the DIRAC/DiracX side, not just individual developers' interests. There was agreement (Andrea, Alan) that this needs a dedicated conversation with the DIRAC/DiracX side once the ADR for the transformation/production system is released; Andrea suggested this conversation would likely happen in September.
- Rucio integration: Liz Sexton-Kennedy reported that Eric (Rucio contact) considers it premature to engage deeply now. As long as the DIRAC "bio-based" (file in / file out, or multiple-files in / multiple-files out) model is followed, the existing Rucio integration is adequate; more complex needs (e.g. luminosity-range-based access rather than pure file-based) are where his expertise would become relevant, which he estimated as roughly half a year out. He is willing to attend meetings and advise in the meantime. Separately, Alan Malta Rodrigues reported that, per discussions with the DIRAC and Belle II teams, Rucio integration in DIRAC today is limited — essentially replacing the DIRAC file catalog with the Rucio file catalog — and is not integrated across all DIRAC subsystems; the DIRAC core team itself does not have deep Rucio knowledge and recommended discussing with Belle II and with Christophe Haen.
- Tier-0: the group's working conclusion was to keep the streamer-file processing / repack step still separated from the main WM path (and, for now, not tightly connected to Rucio), while the rest of the Tier-0 workflow (Express stream and prompt calibration) would go through DiracX, similar to how Monte Carlo samples are handled. The repacker was identified as needing dedicated development attention because it does not fit DIRAC's "N files in, N files out" transformation model (it currently processes a stream of data); Alan Malta Rodrigues suggested it is likely to become an extension to the production system rather than a directly native use case.
- Liz Sexton-Kennedy noted that per the CMSSW conceptual design report, the repacker's role is expanding: beyond transforming streamer files into ROOT-based primary datasets, it will now also format files into "processing units" (each processing unit maps to a file). This is possible because the constraint that a luminosity section cannot be split across files has been lifted (in practice this constraint had already not been respected for years, e.g. in the HLT/minimum-bias case). For data, in Run4, a processing unit and a luminosity section can be treated as equivalent above the Tier-0 level, but a single luminosity section is being sized to fit within a file-size limit (~20GB) for ~99% of data.
- Monte Carlo will no longer use the luminosity-section concept at all starting in Run4; that concept remains only for data. (This resolves an earlier open question about how to handle "processing units" for Monte Carlo.)
- CMS Connect is expected to continue serving as a valid, separate entry point into the HTCondor global pool for simple, non-workflow batch-style job submission (it does not do bookkeeping, job chaining, or splitting). The group (Kevin Lannon, Marco Mascheroni, Alan Malta Rodrigues) converged on the view that DiracX should not try to absorb or replace this use case; more complex, multi-step, or data-management-dependent workflows should go through DiracX, while simple direct-to-global-pool submission should remain outside it.
- Release validation is expected to be treated similarly to a private Monte Carlo case, or alternatively as falling under the more flexible "user analysis" use case, given it does not need to be as rigidly structured as central production.
- On unmerged/intermediate files: the group leaned toward the conclusion that unmerged files will need to be tracked in Rucio going forward (registered similarly to any other file, understood as living temporarily until merged, after which their last replica eventually vanishes), because otherwise there is no way to act on them for cleanup/data-driven scheduling. This was described as a likely large increase in the number of files/records Rucio needs to manage (Alan Malta Rodrigues gave a rough, explicitly unverified estimate of roughly 5x based on typical unmerged-to-merged file size ratios; Todor noted the actual increase in database records might be closer to double per file, though associated API/operation counts could be higher). No firm decision was reached; the group agreed real analytics and input from Rucio experts (Eric, and possibly Maggie) are needed, including how ATLAS/PanDA handles the same problem.
- On DBS global/Phys03 unification: Todor and Kevin Lannon expressed a preference for merging into a single database with a separate table for user data, rather than maintaining the current cross-instance migration service (which they view as a source of ongoing failures/operational overhead). Valentin Kuznetsov did not object to revisiting this but flagged three technical issues that would need to be addressed if the databases are unified: DBS query/scaling performance differences between user and production data, an existing race condition in data injection, and Oracle-side partitioning/performance limits as DBS grows (particularly relevant heading into the high-luminosity era). No final decision was made. Kevin Lannon argued the team should not feel bound to preserve old CRAB/WMAgent-era assumptions purely for consistency, given this is a system transition; Valentin Kuznetsov agreed in principle but noted backward compatibility for existing (e.g. Run 3) analyses constrains what can change. Todor and Kevin favored, as a general direction (not yet decided), migrating old data into the new system/interfaces rather than permanently maintaining two parallel pieces of infrastructure.
- Vijay Chakravarty noted CRAB currently has a growing problem with failing DBS publications, and the team is asking users whether parentage information can stop being added to user datasets to mitigate this; Todor confirmed this is already one of his current tasks. Todor clarified that DBS-to-DBS migration (global vs. Phys03) is a separate mechanism from CRAB's publication step; publication happens at job completion, and migration is triggered afterward as part of the DBS service itself (confirmed by Marco Mascheroni that migration is triggered by the CRAB publication process).

## Discussion

### Effort roundtable and prioritization philosophy
See stated commitments under Decisions/Conclusions. Marco Mascheroni described his own initial approach as picking backlog issues that were simple but useful for ramping up on the DIRAC codebase and conventions (e.g. a ticket about standardizing use of `HTTPException` vs. DIRAC-specific error types), framing this as valuable "service work" during the ramp-up phase. Liz Sexton-Kennedy did not object to service work but stressed that most of a person's committed time should eventually be "top-down directed work" agreed jointly with the DIRAC/DiracX team, not left purely to individual interest, especially given the disparity in existing DIRAC experience between CMS and LHCb. Alan Malta Rodrigues raised a related process concern: if a picked-up ticket turns out to require substantial ramp-up or is poorly described, this should be raised (e.g. with Alexander, the scrum master) rather than absorbed silently, citing his own experience with a ticket that needed re-scoping. Alan Malta Rodrigues also cautioned that tracking effort at the granularity of individual technical-interest areas (as discussed with Valentin) may become significant management overhead once concrete tickets exist; Liz Sexton-Kennedy noted USCMS's use of project-management tooling could help and that this initial round was meant to capture suitability/interest, to later be reconciled with management-assigned priorities and resourcing.

### Tier-0
Discussion (with Alan Malta Rodrigues sharing a workflow diagram) covered which parts of the Tier-0 workflow should run through DiracX. Todor noted CMS's Tier-0 starting point ("raw data") differs from LHCb/DIRAC's assumption, since CMS's repack step occurs a stage earlier than what DIRAC/LHCb consider raw. Liz Sexton-Kennedy said that at a high level (discover files, process, produce/register outputs) the workflow is not fundamentally different, but flagged the storage-manager handshake as the biggest open question, since CMS has historically never needed to apply back-pressure on the storage manager (by provisioning more disk instead) and this mechanism sits outside DIRAC's scope. She proposed looking at how another (unspecified, LHC-adjacent) experiment's storage/online system evolved for their Run 3 in preparation for CMS's Run4. The group also discussed the "Calibration/Alignment/Facility" (CAF) group's interest in running some workflows (e.g. PCL) via DiracX rather than WMCore, and the fact that some latency-critical calibration work (e.g. beam-spot alignment) may migrate into P5/online systems depending on ongoing HLT-upgrade/online negotiations — an area the team needs to continue tracking but not prioritize immediately, per Liz Sexton-Kennedy, unless the storage manager team is independently pushing for near-term work. Alan Malta Rodrigues raised a longer-term risk: if Tier-0 expertise/engagement is deferred too long, Tier-0-specific workflow requirements may not end up native to the production system design and could instead become an after-the-fact CMS extension.

### Monte Carlo production
Discussion covered whether MCM, PMP, and Stats (existing PPD tools for MC request/validation) should be absorbed into the DiracX production system. Liz Sexton-Kennedy said her hope is that these tools go away and their functionality is absorbed, and asked whether anyone had talked to PPD about this recently; Alan Malta Rodrigues recalled a past cross-group meeting (with physics coordination and PPD, including Jen(?) Rock) where the PPD side expressed openness to this, though this was not a recent conversation and not directly with current PPD staff. It remained an open question whether this work would be done by the current group or by bringing in someone currently working on MCM/PMP/Stats. Jen Adelman-McCarthy noted PPD is reportedly planning a major MCM rework, making early coordination valuable. The group also discussed reusing common monitoring infrastructure rather than separate front-ends per system; Valentin Kuznetsov advocated distinguishing OpenTelemetry-based metric collection (which he considers architecturally important to propagate through the transformation system and glideinWMS, not just DiracX's web layer) from visualization tooling (where he thinks it's too early to mandate a specific approach, though MONIT/Grafana is the general CMS/HEP direction). He also cautioned that OpenTelemetry adoption at scale will significantly increase the volume of collected metrics and requires careful toolkit choices (established, widely-used tools rather than less proven frameworks). Separately, generator ("Gen") preparation and grid-pack generation were discussed as a likely CMS-specific extension point: grid packs are generated in a specific CPU/OS environment and are only usable for jobs matching that environment; Kevin Lannon and Alan Malta Rodrigues discussed that the Gen group currently uses Rivet/Rihanna(sp. uncertain in transcript) for this on a limited, non-global-pool cluster, bypassing WM-type tools, and that understanding the Gen group's actual requirements/interfaces is a prerequisite before deciding whether/how this should connect to DiracX, CMS Connect, or a dedicated Condor submit host. Francesco Brivio separately raised whether CMS Connect itself should be harmonized/absorbed as a "fourth" DiracX entry point; Kevin Lannon and Marco Mascheroni argued this depends on workflow complexity — simple direct-to-global-pool submission does not need DiracX, while complex/multi-step/data-dependent work does.

### User analysis (partial; continued day 2)
The discussion focused on why DBS currently requires migrating data between the "global" and "Phys03" instances (a CRAB-produced dataset must be present in the same DBS instance as its parent file to satisfy DBS's parentage constraint), and whether this two-instance model with a migration service should be kept, replaced with a single database (with a separate user-data table), or otherwise redesigned — see Decisions/Conclusions for the substance of that discussion. Vijay Chakravarty's question about publication (distinct from migration) was clarified by Todor and Marco Mascheroni. The meeting was closed before covering the rest of user analysis (e.g. private Monte Carlo, "sandbox"-style custom user workflows, and their relationship to transformation-algorithm design) in depth; a brief exchange (Alan Malta Rodrigues) noted user analysis has strong overlap with MC production and Tier-0 concepts already discussed, that CMS Connect will remain the outlet for workflows that don't need DiracX-level bookkeeping, and that the relationship between MCM-like pre-validation pipelines and the production system's higher-level DAG/chaining role is still unclear and needs further discussion.

- What exactly does the Gen group need from a workflow/interface perspective for grid-pack generation (environment/architecture compatibility, whether Rihanna could generate DIRAC jobs directly, or whether it should stay closer to the global pool/CMS Connect)?
- What is the actual expected scale impact (file count, API/operation load) of registering unmerged/intermediate files in Rucio, and how does ATLAS/PanDA handle the equivalent case? (Eric and possibly Maggie need to weigh in.)
- Should DBS's global and Phys03 instances be unified into a single database (with a separate user-data table), and if so, how are the scaling, data-injection race-condition, and Oracle partitioning concerns Valentin Kuznetsov raised addressed? No decision was reached.
- Who (which specific person/group) will represent PPD in discussions about absorbing MCM/PMP/Stats functionality into the production system, and how does this relate to the production system's own design scope (is MCM-like pre-validation more analogous to LHCb's separate pipeline, or part of the same production-system DAG)?
