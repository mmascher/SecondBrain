# New WM Dev Team Weekly Meeting

## Summary

Alan Malta Rodrigues ran through a Google-document-based agenda covering news items, then led discussion on preparing for the upcoming DIRAC/DiracX workshop in Prague, and the meeting closed with a planning discussion led by Liz Sexton-Kennedy and Kevin Lannon about top-down planning for the DiracX transition.

On news items, Alan noted a CMS Monit ticket (discussed with Carlos, who is busy with other things) aimed at removing the CMS WM Core team e-group from the distribution list of alerts generated by Grafana dashboards (both CMS Web Services / Dublin Core-related and WMAgent alerts). Marco Mascheroni raised a related need: an e-group containing only the people working on the DIRAC integration, so he could grant SSH access to a scheduler connected to the global pool for testing without adding people to the usual glideinWMS distribution (which would expose them to unrelated alerts/emails). Alan confirmed the current CMS WM Core team e-group has only five members (himself, Andrea, Todor, Marco, and until recently Kenji, whom he removed) and already receives WM Core alerts. Liz Sexton-Kennedy suggested it would be clearer to delete the existing e-group and create a new one explicitly named for DIRAC/CMS rather than repurpose the current one; Alan agreed to look into creating the new e-group, noting the process uses a different admin portal. Alan also reviewed and pruned some now-stale entries related to a prior offline-computing-week update from Liz and an old leftover item.

The bulk of the meeting concerned preparing a response to Federico regarding the DIRAC/DiracX workshop in Prague (second week of October). Federico had asked (in a Mattermost thread, with follow-ups from Cedric and others) for community input on what topics should be covered, including plenary content and hacking-session topics. Alan noted Federico's message suggested a CMS-focused contribution explaining why CMS adopted glideinWMS/HTCondor as its workload-management layer rather than DiracX's native submission, and that this session might be a relatively short (~15-20 minute) talk. Liz Sexton-Kennedy read the invitation as effectively asking CMS to explain and justify its architectural choice (community scale, flexibility, existing simple pipelines already built) and suggested Marco would likely be the one presenting it; she noted CMS has given related documents to the LHCC but never formally presented this reasoning to a WLCG or multi-experiment audience.

Separately, Marco Mascheroni proposed that the workshop (or the time around the CMS offline computing week) would be a good opportunity for a longer, informal, whiteboard-style deep-dive on the HTCondor+glideinWMS layer for the benefit of the wider (non-submission-infrastructure) team, since many team members don't have detailed knowledge of this layer despite depending on it. Discussion clarified this would likely need to happen in (at least) two forms: (1) a CMS-internal session, likely around the CMS offline computing week, focused on giving the wider CMS team a working understanding of the Condor/glideinWMS layer; and (2) a separate, more community-oriented session (potentially in parallel with or alongside the DIRAC workshop) aimed at other DIRAC-using experiments considering glideinWMS adoption. Alan suggested reaching out to CMS Level-1/Level-2 management and touching base with Andrea before committing to a specific agenda slot. It was not decided whether the CMS-internal session and the community-facing session would be combined or run separately, nor exactly when the CMS-internal one would occur (offline computing week vs. the following week).

Other candidate workshop topics identified: (1) the DiracX production/transformation system timeline relative to CMS's CSA28 milestone — Liz Sexton-Kennedy flagged this as her main interest and noted it is not explicit on the current agenda, so the group should raise it explicitly; (2) Rucio integration/support within DIRAC — Cedric indicated he is not the right person to speak to this and suggested "Max" as a better candidate, since (per Alan) it was actually Belle II that has invested the most in Rucio integration as DIRAC's data-management/catalog backend; (3) database schema portability, referencing investigation Valentin previously did identifying shortfalls in DIRAC's use of SQLAlchemy as a portability layer that could block schema evolution for experiments needing to extend the database — the group agreed this would be a good topic given Valentin is expected to attend; (4) CWL, since Camille Mauceri and Vijay Chakravarty have been investigating it — Camille has looked briefly at whether CWL could support CMS's private Monte Carlo production via CRAB, finding it plausible but details still unclear pending more information, and Vijay reported he would be presenting later that same day on converting from RECOMGR to CWL (a link was shared in the meeting chat/Mattermost for people to review offline, given limited time).

Marco Mascheroni also suggested that, since the workshop will assume familiarity with the transformation-system ADRs, it would be useful to discuss any open questions or doubts about those ADRs (ideally with input from the DIRAC developers who designed the system) in a whiteboard-style session, and to use the opportunity to plan what CMS will build and where. Alan agreed this would be valuable but was doubtful the group would have time to go through the ADRs in detail before the offline computing week, and both Alan and Marco expressed concern that a finalized version of the relevant ADR(s) may not even be ready by then, especially for the transformation/production-system and CWL-related ADRs, which Alan expects to be particularly large/complex based on his review of an earlier, related ADR. Liz Sexton-Kennedy suggested that if the workshop's hacking sessions are meant for whiteboard-style design discussion (rather than routine biweekly-style hacking), that might be the right venue for an ADR-driven session; Alan agreed this could work but felt CMS also needs a CMS-specific look at the ADRs, distinct from a full-community discussion. Alan planned to write up formatted notes from the meeting (since he did not have access to the transcript) and share them in the Mattermost thread, aiming to send an initial (non-final) response to Federico by early the following week, on the reasoning that earlier feedback gives the DIRAC team more time to accommodate it.

In the final section, Liz Sexton-Kennedy raised feedback from a meeting the previous day (which she noted several attendees, including Alan, had not been able to join) that planning "doesn't go from top-down enough." She stated the one clear top-down constraint she can state is that some handful of workflows need to go through the new (DiracX-based) system, and that after CSA28 there is a top-down expectation of being "release ready," which she interpreted as meaning production start for Monte Carlo for Run 4. She was uncertain whether intermediate Monte Carlo sample generation would be needed between DC27 and CSA28, and said this needs discussion at the offline computing week. She noted there is no Level-2 manager yet to consult, so she was asking the whole group instead for input on the probability/task assumptions currently on the table and any additional top-down-derived tasks. Kevin Lannon described a planning exercise he had done independently (without consulting others): starting from the assumption that CSA28's goal is a complete Monte Carlo workflow chain from no files to NanoAOD output, listing the pieces needed, guessing what can be parallelized, and allocating quarter-sized time blocks working backward from the deadline — explicitly a rough, likely-incomplete first pass. He said he had previously sent Liz a related spreadsheet (originally prepared for a USCMS budget review) that she did not recall receiving or acting on; he agreed to resend it. Kevin proposed that Liz (as the natural owner) write down an explicit plan in this style, that the group then review it to identify missing steps or subsystems, and that going forward the group compare actual quarterly progress against the plan to diagnose whether slippage is due to underestimating complexity, insufficient FTE effort (he stressed tracking FTEs, not just names, since no one works 100% on this), or unplanned work — arguing that without this kind of documented tracking, it will remain difficult to justify requests for additional effort, since past requests have been told the need for more effort has not been demonstrated. Liz agreed she had missed a needed discussion on this and proposed that she and Kevin talk directly and then bring the result back to this meeting for wider input. The transcript ends shortly after, with Alan asking Liz to clarify context from the meeting he had missed the previous day ("top it down").

## Decisions / Conclusions

- The current CMS WM Core team e-group (5 members: Alan Malta Rodrigues, Andrea, Todor, Marco Mascheroni, previously Kenji) will either be renamed/repurposed or replaced by a new e-group, with all alert sources (Grafana/Dublin Core dashboards, WMAgent) removed from whichever group is retained; Liz Sexton-Kennedy's preference was to delete the old group and create a new one with a name explicitly referencing DIRAC/CMS (e.g., "CMS DIRAC"). Alan agreed to investigate creating the new e-group.
- The team agreed a CMS-internal HTCondor/glideinWMS deep-dive/whiteboard session for the wider team is worth pursuing, likely timed around the CMS offline computing week, separate from a more community-facing session aimed at other DIRAC-using experiments (though exact timing/format was not finalized).
- Vijay Chakravarty's RECOMGR-to-CWL conversion work and Camille Mauceri's investigation into CWL for private Monte Carlo with CRAB were identified as relevant, concrete inputs for the DIRAC workshop CWL discussion.
- The team agreed the workshop feedback to Federico does not need to be final/complete — an initial, non-final response by early the following week was judged preferable to a delayed complete one.

- Federico's Mattermost message (workshop organization for Prague, sent the Tuesday before the meeting) drew follow-up responses from Cedric (disclaiming deep Rucio-integration expertise and suggesting "Max" instead) and at least one other person whose feedback Alan did not fully recall.
- Alan noted the Belle II experiment is reportedly the DIRAC-using experiment that has invested the most in Rucio integration as a data-management/data-catalog backend.
- Valentin (on vacation during this meeting) previously investigated DIRAC's database schema and identified potential shortfalls in its use of SQLAlchemy as a portability layer, which could become a blocker if experiments need to extend or change the underlying database schema/classes.
- Camille Mauceri looked briefly into whether CWL could support CMS's private Monte Carlo production workflow via CRAB; found it plausible in principle but was unable to assess implementation details without more information.
