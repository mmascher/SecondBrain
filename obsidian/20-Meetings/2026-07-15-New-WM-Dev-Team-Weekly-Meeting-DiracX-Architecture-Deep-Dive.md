# New WM Dev Team Weekly Meeting: DiracX Architecture Deep Dive

## Summary

This was a long, technical New WM Dev Team weekly meeting (led by Andrea Piccinelli) focused on working through open architectural questions for CMS's adoption of [[DIRACX]]/[[DIRAC]] as the future [[Workload Management]] system, using an agenda carried over from a prior (Monday) meeting. The group first discussed staffing/organizational implications of integrating operations tools into the WM/DevOps workflow, then spent most of the meeting on a whiteboard-driven architecture discussion (led by Andrea and Alan Malta Rodrigues, with input from Marco Mascheroni, Valentin Kuznetsov, Todor, and Kevin Lannon) about how DiracX's production system, transformation system, and workload management system relate to each other and to CMS's existing [[Submission Infrastructure]] (HTCondor access points, [[glideinWMS]]). Much of the discussion concerned where a CMS-specific "job materialization" component (translating DiracX task-database records into HTCondor jobs at CMS access points) should live, without reaching a final decision. The meeting also covered resource-status/monitoring integration, [[DBS]]/[[Rucio]] interaction and output bookkeeping, and secrets management, surfacing many open questions to be taken back to the core DIRAC/DiracX development team. The transcript's caption capture ends mid-discussion, before what appeared to be a natural close of the meeting.

## Decisions / Conclusions

- Interaction between [[glideinWMS]]/HTCondor and DiracX components should continue to happen only through HTCondor access points; the group converged on **not** adding new direct links between glideinWMS and DiracX systems (e.g. the transformation system), in order to preserve modularity and avoid unnecessary cross-dependencies. Marco Mascheroni and Andrea Piccinelli both endorsed this.
- The DIRAC/DiracX team is currently prioritizing the production and transformation systems over the workload management system, which Andrea Piccinelli and Liz Sexton-Kennedy noted works in CMS's favor, since CMS's own priority is also the production/transformation layer.
- The full "microagent" concept discussed in March (a process on the HTCondor scheduler watching a group of jobs, tracking status/resubmissions, and managing merges) is not currently being pursued as originally envisioned. Marco Mascheroni's assessed position: it is too early to rule it out completely, since a partial version (e.g. a microagent-like process updating the task database from the scheduler) might still be needed depending on how DiracX's own job-status/retry handling works out.
- Task creation is the transformation system's responsibility; task **state** management (e.g. handling retries when a task/job fails, making the associated input data available again) is the responsibility of the next-layer system (workload management or data management), which reacts to state changes the transformation system's input plugins pick up. This division of responsibility was largely agreed among Andrea Piccinelli and Alan Malta Rodrigues, though downstream cleanup ownership (of task/production database records) remained unresolved (see Open Questions).
- Regarding data management: per DiracX's design, data-management operations are represented as another transformation within a production (confirmed by Kevin Lannon from the DIRAC hackathon material) and are routed to DIRAC's Request(Management) System rather than to the workload management system — this is the "bridge" between the transformation system and [[Rucio]] that Andrea Piccinelli had been looking for.
- The DiracX Configuration System is **not** already complete, correcting an earlier assumption; Alan Malta Rodrigues learned during the hackathon that only a few capabilities (e.g. token authentication/authorization) currently exist there, and it needs to be updated accordingly in CMS's system-inventory document.

## Discussion

### Operations tools integration and staffing
Andrea Piccinelli opened with a carried-over agenda item on integrating operations and monitoring tools more tightly with the WM infrastructure, noting this would need coordination with the current ops team, especially as HTCondor/glideinWMS DevOps and WM DevOps areas are expected to merge — not immediately, but "in the near future." Liz Sexton-Kennedy connected this to broader plans (from a recent face-to-face) for development and operations teams to move toward a more unified DevOps-style organization, though she was unsure of the concrete plans. Jen Adelman-McCarthy raised a staffing concern: recent hires for the PRP/CERN-based roles (referred to in the transcript as "sign O," "Hasanamad"/Hassan Ahmed, and Gregor — names as transcribed, with some uncertainty) were specifically hired for DevOps work, enabling tools that make operations easier; Gregor currently maintains WMCore while the departing developer (transcribed as "Sana Bed"/"sauna bed," name unclear) has been doing most of the rapid DevOps tool development. Since a replacement hire is imminent and the associated job description has not yet been finalized, Jen suggested this is the moment to consider whether the new hire should split time (e.g. 50%) doing DevOps work for DIRAC, potentially collaborating with LHCb's operations people at CERN on similar needs. Andrea Piccinelli separately noted the practice of getting operator feedback on new WM features (an MVP-style feedback loop with operators) as a useful thing to keep during the transition.

Alan Malta Rodrigues added that core operational capabilities currently relied upon by ops tools (e.g. state-machine automation not depending on external sources, and easily searchable/queryable logging — described as a long-standing pain point) should be built into DiracX itself rather than bolted on afterward as separate tools that never converge with the main system. He also recalled an old WMCore/Request Manager 2 design idea — a "sandbox" allowing an operator to drop in a script as a pluggable, operations-driven extension — as a pattern potentially worth considering for DiracX. Andrea Piccinelli noted the team will need to continuously evaluate, as DiracX development progresses, where logging/observability needs to be wired in versus deferred until a working prototype exists, given limited person-power.

### DiracX system inventory and prioritization
Alan Malta Rodrigues shared a system diagram originally created for this year's Offline Computing Week (roughly three months old at the time of this meeting) showing DIRAC's/DiracX's core system blocks: production system, transformation system, workload management system, request management system, and data management system, plus a resource status system (comparable in role to CMS's [[CRIC]]). Some systems were shown with a tentative "shift to 2029" placeholder, reflecting that timeline's age and the fact that DIRAC core did not yet have a firm view on when those systems would start in DiracX. Liz Sexton-Kennedy asked whether this reflected what the DIRAC team would prioritize absent CMS's involvement; Alan agreed that was the prioritization before CMS/other communities joined, and that renewed prioritization discussions are expected. Liz also asked whether the transformation vs. workload management priority order might flip once CMS's own WMS-interfacing work is more mature; Andrea Piccinelli confirmed the DIRAC/DiracX team is currently prioritizing transformation and production systems, which aligns well with CMS's needs.

### Microagent concept re-evaluation
Following up on a concept discussed in March, Alan Malta Rodrigues asked whether the group was now leaning against including a CMS-specific "microagent" (a process watching a group of scheduler jobs, tracking status/retries, and managing merges). Andrea Piccinelli said the plan is to first understand how HTCondor and DiracX can work together, then revisit the concept. Marco Mascheroni elaborated that the full microagent concept probably isn't needed if the DiracX/transformation framework already provides equivalent functionality (e.g. registering worker-node output status), but a partial version — e.g. a small scheduler-side process updating the task database — might still be useful; he was not ready to rule it out entirely.

### Task/production/transformation system architecture (whiteboard discussion)
Using a shared whiteboard sketch (originally built for a prior CMS-led DiracX proposal), Andrea Piccinelli and Alan Malta Rodrigues worked through what functionality the production and transformation systems provide, referencing a DIRAC hackathon presentation by Chris Burr (transcribed at points as "Chris Bear"/"CRISPR," slides 14 and 19).

- **Production system role (unresolved):** Marco Mascheroni asked what the production system does beyond accepting a request and breaking it into transformations submitted to the transformation system, noting he has not studied it directly. Alan Malta Rodrigues's understanding: it accepts a CWL "production" document (a DAG with step and data dependencies), and breaks this down into database records that the transformation system acts on; further functionality is unclear and, per Alan, likely will not be clarified until around August. Valentin Kuznetsov's view was more minimal: architecturally, the production system is essentially a REST/POST web-service front end that persists an incoming CWL/JSON request to a database, with the transformation system reading it back and doing the actual work — otherwise he sees little business logic residing in the production system itself. Kevin Lannon noted that materials from the hackathon (slide 14) suggest the production system is mainly about chaining/coordinating transformations.
- **Task creation model:** The transformation system creates "tasks" — job-like database records — using per-transformation input plugins that group input data (LFNs/Lumis for CMS, potentially other units later) according to a defined rule; these are written to a task database (a table, likely in the same Postgres-based DIRAC core database, though this was uncertain). Marco Mascheroni described how, in the current WMS model, a downstream component would look at tasks and create a corresponding HTCondor job, updating task status back — a component that does not yet exist and would need to be built for CMS.
- **Scale differences:** Alan Malta Rodrigues highlighted a key architectural difference from native DIRAC: DIRAC's pilot mechanism defines the scale of submission (one pilot per resource slot), whereas CMS's model materializes each database row as close to directly into the HTCondor pool as possible. Marco Mascheroni noted HTCondor's late-materialization feature could support this at scale, though more scale testing is needed (e.g. memory footprint differences between running and idle jobs).
- **Push/pull vs. pub/sub:** Valentin Kuznetsov described two general architectural choices for how a front-end (production system) hands work to a back-end (transformation system): push/pull (persisted requests requiring later cleanup) versus pub/sub (no persistence, but weaker delivery guarantees). He assessed the DiracX team is likely using the push/pull model, which would make it architecturally similar to what CMS already does in WMCore, differing mainly in implementation.
- **Cleanup/task-state ownership (unresolved):** Valentin Kuznetsov asked who is responsible for cleaning up production/task database records once processing completes. Marco Mascheroni suggested the transformation system likely owns this, but did not want to speculate further. Alan Malta Rodrigues proposed the production system might track when an entire production is complete and propagate that information to other systems (e.g. the transformation system deleting no-longer-needed transformation records), but stressed this is speculative pending clarification. Andrea Piccinelli connected this to task retry handling: if a task/job fails, associated input is returned to the pool of unprocessed input for a transformation to pick up again — task creation belongs to the transformation system, but task lifecycle/retry management belongs to whichever downstream system (WMS or data management) is running the tasks.

### Placement of the CMS job-materialization component ("point 5")
A large portion of the meeting concerned where the CMS-specific component that turns task-database entries into HTCondor jobs submitted at access points should live. Todor and Alan Malta Rodrigues jointly framed **three options**:
1. Gliding-WMS-style pilot functionality becomes a **core DIRAC/DiracX capability**, available to everyone with no CMS-specific code — but this is a community/consortium decision, not one CMS can make unilaterally.
2. The component is built as an **extension/plugin of an existing DiracX system block** (most likely the transformation system or the workload management system).
3. The component is built as a **standalone solution outside both DiracX and any CMS-specific DIRAC extension** — e.g. living on the glideinWMS/HTCondor side, querying the DiracX task database directly.

Todor argued for option 3: it produces a self-contained, more easily "sellable" alternative pilot system to the wider DIRAC consortium, and avoids forcing any future adopter of the CMS-style HTCondor/glideinWMS pilot mechanism to also depend on CMS-specific plugins living inside the transformation system (currently the only DIRAC system block with a plugin mechanism). He noted this comes at the cost of a more rigid, harder-to-evolve solution compared to embedding it as a plugin. Alan Malta Rodrigues leaned toward option 2 (an extension of the workload management system specifically), reasoning that CMS will already need DiracX extensions elsewhere, that reusing DIRAC's own modularization mechanism is an easier path to eventual consortium-level adoption, and that the workload management system is one of the only systems DIRAC core is actively designing right now — raising the action item to check whether there is still time to request such an extension point.

Valentin Kuznetsov reframed the discussion around **"where" vs. "how"**: regardless of which system hosts the CMS component, DiracX must expose a well-defined, pluggable abstraction layer (with a configuration switch) that CMS's extension satisfies — otherwise "extension" is meaningless. Todor confirmed the DIRAC/DiracX team stated during the hackathon that they will provide such a configurable switch to flip between pilot mechanisms, meaning the "how" commitment is implicitly already there; what remains open is strictly "where." Valentin argued CMS should proactively design the "how" (the API) once "where" is settled, to avoid rework, and suggested this design work should happen soon rather than after DiracX's own design solidifies.

Andrea Piccinelli and Alan Malta Rodrigues disagreed on process: Andrea felt CMS lacks sufficient DiracX expertise to responsibly choose a placement without first hearing DiracX developers' constraints, citing a recurring CMS pattern of independently brainstorming and then having to walk conclusions back after talking to system owners; Alan agreed communication with DiracX experts is essential but felt CMS should still arrive at and bring its own proposed placement (based on CMS's own architecture and needs) into that conversation, rather than asking the DIRAC team to decide unilaterally. No resolution was reached on process or on which of the three options to pursue; the group agreed a dedicated discussion with core DiracX developers (e.g. Chris Burr, Federico) is needed, which Marco Mascheroni agreed to help arrange.

### Resource status system and CMS resource monitoring
Discussing agenda topic 6, the group considered how DiracX's Resource Status System maps onto CMS's existing resource-information stack: [[CRIC]] (sites, storage elements, compute elements), Submission Infrastructure Grafana dashboards fed by pilot-based resource discovery (GPUs, versions, etc.), pilot sizing (whole-node vs. 8-core pilots), and SAM/HammerCloud-style probing tests run by the site support team that drive decisions to enable/disable sites or place them in downtime, which in turn affects work routing. Alan Malta Rodrigues framed the open question as needing discussion with the core DIRAC team about whether/how the Resource Status System can support CMS's existing data sources (e.g. via extensions or plugins) rather than replacing them outright. Liz Sexton-Kennedy separately asked whether DIRAC/WLCG has an equivalent of SAM tests (raising WLCG's ETF framework as a possible reference point); this was not answered and remained an open question she wanted to follow up on.

Valentin Kuznetsov raised a related monitoring dilemma: if CMS promotes HTCondor+glideinWMS as a general pilot option for other DIRAC consortium members, CMS's current monitoring approach (a custom scraper querying HTCondor classads into CMS Monitoring/Grafana) is CMS-specific and would not generalize out of the box, partly because HTCondor classads have no enforced schema (each client can define arbitrary key/value metrics), making generic monitoring difficult without a documented convention. He suggested this may require direct engagement with the HTCondor development community to define a schema/approach that other consortium members could use out of the box. Marco Mascheroni agreed CMS's multi-year Grafana/monitoring expertise could benefit other experiments if generalized, noted HTCondor already provides a `condor_adstash`-based mechanism CMS does not currently use, and mentioned that Carlos is currently refactoring CMS's scraper (with Valentin consulting) — a plausible starting point for that broader conversation, though no next step was formally agreed.

### DBS/Rucio interaction and output bookkeeping
Andrea Piccinelli asked how DBS/Rucio interaction fits the architecture, given that data-management operations are represented as transformations within a production. Kevin Lannon confirmed (from the hackathon material) that such data-management steps are routed to DIRAC's Request(Management) System — DIRAC's rough equivalent of CMS's microservices/request-queue pattern — appearing as a step in the CWL. Alan Malta Rodrigues confirmed the transformation system writes records to a database, and downstream systems (workload management for job-type records, request management for data-type records) act on them independently — the "bridge" Andrea had been looking for.

Valentin Kuznetsov raised an open question about whether this transformation-system-to-request-management-system flow is strictly one-way (push), or whether CMS would also need to query it (e.g. to look up dataset parentage in DBS/Rucio before generating tasks) — the actual requirement was not established.

Todor highlighted a more fundamental conceptual difference between CMS's current approach and DiracX's: DIRAC's transformation system distributes data for an entire workflow up front rather than in per-task chunks (CMS's current WorkQueueElement-style model), with that placement handled by the resource management system. He judged the "forward path" — where the initial DBS lookup/data-discovery step happens (inside the transformation system's splitting/plugin logic) — is reasonably well understood. He explicitly flagged the "backward path" (how/where output data gets registered with the bookkeeping/data-catalog system after being produced and moved) as the more troubling, currently unanswered part of the design; he stated he has no answer to it. Marco Mascheroni confirmed this open question ("where/how to register job output into the metadata catalog / Rucio file catalog, and whether on the worker node or centrally") also came up during the hackathon and remains a "hot topic."

Liz Sexton-Kennedy asked whether anyone had relevant experience indicating which approach (worker-node vs. centralized registration) is better. Marco Mascheroni argued this is mainly a scale question: centralized registration allows batched updates (which Rucio/DBS might prefer for scalability), while worker-node registration reduces the window of "dark data" (output unknown to the catalog); he noted the total number of database inserts is the same either way. Valentin Kuznetsov strongly cautioned against uncontrolled worker-node calls to critical central services, citing a past incident where simultaneous worker-node queries against DAS caused denial-of-service-like conditions — tolerable for DAS (non-critical, can be shut down) but not for Rucio/DBS. Andrea Piccinelli agreed CMS would need some protective/throttling mechanism to prevent user workflows from embedding uncontrolled DBS calls, referencing a past incident (naming unclear in the transcript) affecting production/TPS-related systems. Valentin Kuznetsov proposed that any worker-node registration approach must first be evaluated against realistic worst-case throughput requirements in consultation with Rucio/DBS service maintainers. Liz Sexton-Kennedy suggested ATLAS (a heavy user of Rucio's metadata/file-location system, alongside AMI as their primary catalog) might have useful real-world throughput data to inform this. No conclusion was reached.

### Secrets management
Marco Mascheroni noted he has not seen a secrets-management mechanism in the DIRAC framework, which will become necessary once CMS needs to hold and use certificates/tokens to contact external services (e.g. Rucio, DBS) from DiracX-based components. He flagged this as a topic to raise with the DIRAC/DiracX team; Andrea Piccinelli agreed this is likely a common concern across DIRAC's user communities.
